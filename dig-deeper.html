DigDeeper

A dig-down mining game with an old field-guide look. Steer a dwarf miner through eight layers of rock and out to the left and right, mine shiny ore and gems, haul them home to sell, and upgrade every tool so you can dig deeper still.

How to play

  1. Dig. Hold and drag down, left or right to dig that way. On a computer, the arrow keys work too. Going down leaves nothing behind, so to come back up you'll need a rope (see below).
  2. Mine. Shiny rocks are ore and gems. Walk up to one and keep holding toward it: your dwarf swings the pick until it pops. Harder minerals take more swings. On a phone you can also press and hold the rock itself, and rocks diagonally next to you mine with a tap.
  3. Fill your pack. Everything you mine streams into your backpack. The bar at the top shows how full it is.
  4. Go home. Climb back up your ropes, or press the big Home button and your dwarf walks to camp the quickest way along your ropes by itself. Going down, hold down on a rope or ladder to slide.
  5. Unload and sell. Step onto the landing pad yourself and your pack empties into the tank. (Home stops at camp but never unloads for you, so you can keep loot in your pack for contracts or later.) Then tap the camp building at the top (look for the "Tap for Camp" sign) and visit the Trader to sell what's in the tank for coins.
  6. Upgrade. At Equipment, spend coins and minerals on better digging picks, mining picks, packs, lamps, lifts and senders, then dig deeper for better finds.

You can't dig plain rock upward, only down and to the sides, but you can mine a mineral right above you by holding up. To go up you climb a rope. Coins and depth sit at the top of the screen, next to the pack bar, the Sound switch and the ↺ button that starts a brand-new game. Under them is today's daily goal. The first time you find each mineral, a specimen card from your field journal pops up.

Your first 10 minutes

You start at the surface with an Excavation Pick for digging, a Rusty Pick for mining, a small Sack that holds 20, five support beams, one stick of dynamite, 4 climbing ropes and 2 wall lanterns. Topsoil is soft, so dig straight down a few metres and start tunnelling sideways.

  1. Look for coal (dark grey) and copper (orange) near the top. Hold toward each one until it pops into your pack.
  2. When the pack is full, head home and step on the pad. Then tap the camp building, walk to the Trader and sell, keeping 3 copper back.
  3. Your first goal is the Steel Mattock: 35 coins and 3 copper, at Equipment. It digs faster and can handle the Gravel layer below the clay.
  4. Next, the Canvas Pack (60 coins) doubles what you can carry on each trip.
  5. Below 18 m you'll find quartz, and from 30 m tin. Below 45 m comes iron, which you need for the Tempered Mattock, the Iron Pick and your first bars.

Every 25 m you go deeper than ever before, the depth pops up on screen, and every new 100 m record pays coins equal to the depth.

Layers and minerals

The ground is made of eight layers, each one harder than the last. Your digging pick decides which layers you can dig. One layer too hard and digging is very slow; two layers too hard and you can't dig at all, so you'll need a better digging pick first.

  • Topsoil (0 to 25 m) and Clay (to 75 m): the Excavation Pick you start with digs both.
  • Gravel (to 150 m): the Steel Mattock.
  • Limestone (to 300 m): the Tempered Mattock.
  • Granite (to 500 m): the Granite Pick.
  • Basalt (to 800 m): the Heavy Rock Pick.
  • Crystal Caverns (to 1,200 m): the Crystal Pick. Full of open caves.
  • Magma Rock (1,200 m and down, with no bottom): the Magma Pick.

Minerals get rarer and more valuable the deeper you go. Each one starts at a set depth: coal and copper near the top, then quartz, tin and iron, amethyst from 90 m, silver from 150 m, topaz from 200 m, gold from 300 m, emerald from 350 m, sapphire from 500 m, platinum from 600 m, ruby from 700 m, cobalt from 900 m and diamond from 1,000 m. Gems are light and worth a lot; ore is heavy but you need it for upgrades.

Veins

Every now and then you'll spot a vein: a glittering streak of one mineral that winds through the rock and sometimes forks. Veins are rare, but each tile in one holds a fat clump. The deeper the vein, the better the mineral in it, so a vein at 100 m might be copper or iron while one near 1,000 m can be cobalt, platinum or even diamond. The first time you mine into a vein, the game tells you to follow the glitter.

Caves, chests and geodes

Below 12 m the ground has open caves. From 15 m, some caves hide a treasure chest: walk up to it and keep holding toward it: your dwarf pries it open in three swings, for coins, and sometimes a geode, dynamite or beams. Geodes also turn up in the rock from 40 m. Take them to the Jeweler at Camp and crack them open for gems.

Fossils and relics

Rare finds like snail shells, fish bones, an ancient coin, a mammoth tusk or a dino skull go to the museum at Camp the first time you bring each one home. Any repeats land in the tank, and the Trader buys them for coins.

Cave-ins

From 25 m down, a long tunnel can collapse. If you dig a sideways run 5 or more tiles wide, the ceiling starts creaking and the screen shakes. Tap the 🪵 button to place a support beam; one beam holds up to 7 tiles. A beam needs rock right above it, so it can't go under an open shaft or cave. If you ignore it, part of the tunnel fills with rubble. Rubble is soft, so you can dig right through it, but you lose the open path. Beams cost 20 coins for five at the Outfitter.

Dynamite

Tap 🧨 to light a stick. It blasts a 3 by 3 hole around you, and any ore in it pops loose. Dynamite costs 30 coins at the Outfitter, and chests sometimes have a couple.

Ropes and lanterns

Climbing ropes

Digging down, dropping into holes and falling leave nothing behind, so think before you drop. To climb back up, tap the 🪢 rope button (R on a computer): a grapple flies straight up until it bites into the first rock above, however far that is, and the rope hangs down to you. Hold up to climb it.

  • Ropes stay where you shoot them, so rig your main shaft once and it's free from then on. A rope that stops above you still works: climb up from right under it.
  • You start with 4 ropes. The Outfitter sells Climbing Ropes ×3 for 15 coins.
  • The Pulley Lift and Elevator ride straight shafts without ropes, so they save ropes too.
  • Long falls hurt: dropping more than 5 tiles leaves your dwarf dazed for a moment.
  • Stuck with no ropes? Press Home: if there's no way up and you have none left, your dwarf finds a spare rope in their boot.

Wall lanterns

The deeper you go, the darker it gets: it is half dark by about 25 m, and by about 40 m only your head lamp lights the way, and it keeps getting darker deep down. Tap the 🏮 lantern button (L) to hang a wall lantern on any tile you've dug. It lights a warm circle about 3.5 tiles across for good, so you can see the ore, gems, veins and drops around it even after you walk away. Lanterns are permanent once hung and need no fuel.

  • You start with 2 lanterns. The Outfitter sells Wall Lanterns ×3 for 30 coins.
  • Hang them along your main shaft and the long tunnels you use, and your way home stays lit.
  • If a tunnel caves in on a lantern, it drops back into your supplies.

Left, right and the walls

The mine is 100 columns wide, with your camp in the middle. Walk or dig toward the edge of the screen and it scrolls along with you. The same layers carry on to both sides, but each side has its own riches:

  • To the left (west): metal country. Copper, tin, iron, silver, gold, platinum and cobalt are much more common, and veins are almost always metal.
  • To the right (east): gem country. Quartz, amethyst, topaz, emerald, sapphire, ruby and diamond are much more common, and most veins are gems.
  • Under camp: a mix of both, as before.

The change builds up over the first 10 columns past camp. Solid bedrock walls mark the ends, 44 columns to the left and 43 to the right. When camp is off screen, a small sign at the edge points home with the distance, like "🏠 23 m ▶", and the Home button works from anywhere, even out on the grass. The catch is the walk: the further out you dig, the longer every trip home with a full pack.

Food and water

Digging is hungry, thirsty work. Two bars under the pack bar show your 💧 water and 🍞 food. They only go down while you're underground, never at camp and never while the game is closed.

  • What drains them: digging and mining use the most, walking less, standing still least. The deeper you are, the faster they drop, and water goes extra fast in the hot Magma Rock and the dry Crystal Caverns. Near the top, full bars last a few minutes of digging.
  • Refilling: nothing refills by itself. Buy a Water Flask (+50 water, 2 coins) or Trail Bread (+50 food, 3 coins) at the Cook's kitchen and use it with the 💧 and 🍞 buttons, anywhere. In the kitchen, Top up buys and uses exactly enough to fill both bars in one tap.
  • Carrying supplies: each flask or bread takes half a space in your pack, so packing more means room for less loot.
  • Running low: the bars flash red at a quarter full. With no water or no food, digging and mining take twice as long. With neither, your dwarf is too worn out to dig until they eat or drink, but you can still walk, climb and press Home. You never lose anything.
  • Starting out: you begin with full bars plus 3 flasks and 3 bread.

The surface and Field Camp

At the top of the mine you'll find the loot tank and the landing pad. Tap the camp building next to them (it has a "Tap for Camp" sign when you are standing at the top) to walk into Field Camp: two rows of buildings on either side of a road. Hold or drag to walk your dwarf around, or tap a building and your dwarf walks over and opens it. Every building has a keeper who greets you. A gold ! on a building means something is waiting there, like bars to collect, a cut gem that's ready, a contract you can deliver or coal in the kiln. Chimneys smoke while the smelter and the kiln are working. To go back down, walk to the mine entrance and tap it.

The daily goal sits in a banner at the top of camp: one goal a day, like mining a number of one mineral, reaching a depth, unloading three times or opening a chest. Tap Claim when it's done. Coming back on the next day builds a streak that raises the reward by up to 50%.

The tank

The tank has a size limit, shown by the glass gauge on its side (green, then yellow, then red when it's nearly full). When you step on the pad, the most valuable loot goes in first. If it's full, whatever doesn't fit stays in your pack until you sell. The bucket and tube won't send a pack that doesn't fit either. Buy bigger tanks at the Workshop:

  • Rain Barrel (you start with it): holds 60.
  • Copper Tank, 150 coins: holds 150.
  • Riveted Vat, 1,200 coins and 4 bronze bars: holds 350.
  • Iron Cistern, 6,000 coins and 20 iron: holds 800.
  • Steam Silo, 20,000 coins and 24 silver: holds 1,800.
  • Grand Reservoir, 60,000 coins, 30 silver and 6 rubies: holds 4,000.

Every bigger tank also pays a bulk bonus when you use Sell all at the Trader: +3% for the Copper Tank, up to +15% for the Grand Reservoir.

The buildings

  • Trader's stall (Old Brannoc): sell anything in the tank. Each row shows exactly what you'll get, and the chalkboard names today's hot mineral, which sells for 25% more. Sell everything sells the whole tank, even what your next upgrade needs; to keep some back, sell row by row (rows marked 🔒 hold back what an upgrade needs). Once the Charcoal Kiln is built, the Trader also sells firewood.
  • Outfitter: support beams, climbing ropes, wall lanterns and dynamite.
  • Workshop (Hilde the smith): Equipment, with seven lines of upgrades (below). Each upgrade costs coins plus minerals or bars from your tank.
  • Smelter (Bram): tap ore to drop it into the bar mould. When the mould holds a full recipe, the furnace takes it, up to 5 batches in a row. A Bronze Bar takes 1 copper, 1 tin and 1 coal. An Iron Bar takes 2 iron and 1 coal. Silver and Gold Bars take 2 of their ore and 1 coal. Smelting keeps going while you dig and even while the game is closed. Finished bars wait in a tray until you tap Collect.
  • Jeweler (Opal the gem-cutter): tap a geode 4 times to crack it open. Before you start, the panel shows what it could hold, and Crack all opens a whole stack. At the cutting bench, pay a fee equal to a gem's value and wait (from about 15 seconds for an amethyst to a few minutes for a diamond). The cut gem sells for 3 times as much. Up to 3 gems can wait on the bench, and cutting keeps going while you're away.
  • Cook's kitchen (Marta): Top up fills your food and water in one tap, and you can buy Water Flasks and Trail Bread here. The kitchen also serves hot meals that fill your food bar and give you a boost for 4 minutes of digging: Miner's Stew (dig 15% faster), Spiced Pie (+1 pick power) and Prospector's Broth (each rock has a 30% chance to give one extra piece). Meals cost more the deeper you've been, from about 40 coins near the top to several hundred deep down.
    • Miner's Feast: all three boosts at once, for 20% less than buying them one by one.
    • Pack lunches: buy a meal to go for 10% more. It takes 1 space in your pack, and you eat it underground with the 🥡 button whenever you want the boost.
  • Museum (Professor Pell): three tabs. Shelves shows your fossils and relics. Minerals and Fossils are journals of everything you've found, so tap a tile for its full picture, and missing pieces show the depths to look at. Filling a whole shelf gives a lasting bonus: the Sea Shelf adds 10% to sell prices, Lost & Found adds 15% backpack room, and Beast Bones adds 1 pick power. There's also one mystery shelf.
    • Restoration: pay to restore a donated find, then wait (two at a time, and it keeps going while you're away). Restore every piece on a shelf and its bonus gets stronger: 15% on sell prices, 25% more backpack room, or +2 pick power.
  • Contract board (Wren the guild clerk): orders from the town and your Guild rank. See Contracts below.
  • Decorator (Tilly): pieces for your camp, Camp Charm and a weekly special. See below.
  • Charcoal Kiln (Old Soot): build it on its plot for 800 coins. It burns 2 firewood into 1 coal in 20 seconds, up to 10 batches at once, and keeps burning while you dig and while you're away. Collect the coal into your tank. Coal only turns up in the ground down to 500 m, so deep down the kiln is how you keep smelting bars and fuelling a Miner Bot. Firewood costs 2 coins at the Trader, or pick up the old mine timbers lying on some cave floors (walk over a pile for 2 firewood).
  • Bot Garage: builds and upgrades your Miner Bot. See below.
  • Surveyor's Office: still an empty plot. It's where new dig sites will open one day.

The seven upgrade lines

  • Digging picks (7): dig faster and through harder layers, from the Excavation Pick to the Magma Pick.
  • Mining picks (5): fewer swings per rock, plus lucky critical hits. The Sonic Pick also hits the rocks touching the one you mine.
  • Packs (5): carry 20, 40, 75, 130 and then 220.
  • Lamps (3): see further in the dark.
  • Lifts (2): the Pulley Lift carries you up any straight open shaft. The Elevator is much faster.
  • Senders (2): the Rope & Bucket sends your full pack up a straight open shaft without you. The Pneumatic Tube fires it home from anywhere, every 10 seconds.
  • Tanks (6): hold more loot and pay a bigger bonus on Sell all (see above).

The Decorator and Camp Charm

Tilly sells pieces that stand along the road between the buildings, from 300 to 1,000,000 coins. They come in three tiers, and each tier opens when you've placed 3 pieces from the one before:

  • Homely: lantern posts, a timber bench, flower boxes, a camp flag and a stone well. Each adds 1 charm.
  • Proper camp: an ore cart display, a shift bell, gem lamps, a fountain and a bronze dwarf statue. Each adds 2 charm.
  • Grand: a crystal arch, a golden dwarf statue, a clock tower and a diamond fountain. Each adds 3 charm.

Your Camp Charm brings small bonuses:

  • Cozy (4 charm): the daily goal pays 20% more.
  • Welcoming (10): visitors leave a tip when you come up to camp, once an hour.
  • Charming (18): delivered contracts pay 5% more.
  • Famous (27): the daily goal pays 40% more, contracts 10% more, and tips are doubled.
  • Paint jobs: repaint most pieces in Ruby, Rose, Amethyst, Sapphire, Teal or Emerald. A coat costs 8% of the piece's price (at least 200 coins), and going back to the original colours is free.
  • Weekly special: each week (Monday to Sunday) one special piece is for sale, for 6,000 coins and 2 charm: a golden anvil, a glowcap mushroom ring, a pumpkin cart, a snow dwarf, a maypole or a lucky horseshoe. Once bought, it's yours to keep.
  • Tap your pieces in camp: the bell rings, the lanterns flare up, the gem lamps chime and the clock tells you the time. Toss 1 coin into a fountain for one wish a day: some coins, a packed lunch or a geode. The diamond fountain grants bigger wishes.

The Miner Bot

The Bot Garage opens once you reach 300 m. It costs 10,000 coins and 10 Iron Bars, and comes with a clockwork Miner Bot that you can name.

  • Sending it to work: go underground and tap the 🤖 button. Where you're standing becomes its work spot. The bot digs real tunnels there, mines the ore it meets and carries a full hopper up to your tank by itself. When the spot runs dry, it moves on to fresh rock at the same depth and then a little deeper, but never below your deepest depth.
  • Coal: it burns 1 coal for every 3 minutes of work, loaded from your tank each time it leaves camp. If it runs out, or your tank is full, it waits in the garage and a ! appears on the building.
  • Only while you play: the bot works while the game is open. It's slower than you, earning very roughly a fifth to two-fifths of what you'd make at the same depth.
  • Getting stuck: now and then it gets stuck and waves for help. Tap it, or help it from the garage, and it gives you a few coins to say thanks.
  • Upgrades: five, with 4 levels each (2,000, 6,000, 15,000 and 40,000 coins, plus bronze, iron, silver or gold bars). The Drill digs harder rock, from limestone at the start up to the crystal caverns, but always stays one tier behind your own digging pick. Gears make it faster, the Hopper carries 10 finds a trip at first and up to 48, the Fuel tank holds 3 coal at first and up to 16, and Smarts lets it spot ore 1 to 3 rows away and steer towards it.
  • Call home: the garage has buttons to call the bot home and to send it back to its work spot.

Contracts

Wren the guild clerk pins optional orders from the town on the Contract board, like "The bakery wants 12 Coal" or, deeper down, "The mint wants 6 Gold Bars". Each card shows who's asking, a chip for each mineral with a ✓ when your tank has enough, the pay and how much more that is than selling the same loot.

  • Three offers at a time, picked from what you can reach at your deepest depth. Tap Skip to swap one out. Untaken offers are replaced after 30 minutes.
  • Take up to two. Each has a real-time deadline of 1 to 3 hours. If one runs out, it simply lapses: you lose nothing.
  • ⭐ Rush orders: about 1 in 5 offers is a rush job, with only 20 to 40 minutes to deliver but 1.5 times the pay.
  • Deliver from your tank, all at once or a bit at a time. Unload your loot on the pad first, then tap Deliver.
  • The pay: about twice what the loot would sell for near the top, settling to about a quarter more by 1,000 m.
  • Guild rank: every 5 contracts you deliver raises your rank: Prospector, Digger, Seam Finder, Foreman, Shaft Boss, Vein Master, Deep Captain and Master Miner. Each rank adds 2% to contract pay and makes orders about 15% bigger. From Seam Finder on, some orders ask for cut gems from the Jeweler. Every rank-up comes with a one-time coin gift from the Guild that grows with your rank and your deepest depth.

Strategy

Early game: Topsoil to Gravel

  • Buy the Steel Mattock first, then the Canvas Pack. Once your pack holds more than your tank, pick up the Copper Tank. A bigger pack means fewer trips home.
  • Dig one straight shaft down from near the pad and mine sideways from it. Short trips home are quicker.
  • The Rope & Bucket (150 coins and 4 iron) lets you send a full pack up your straight shaft and keep digging.
  • Save iron for the Tempered Mattock (8 iron) and the Iron Pick (5 iron). The metal side to the left makes iron easier to find.

Middle game: Limestone and Granite

  • Start smelting. The Granite Pick needs Iron and Bronze Bars, and the Miner's Rig pack needs Bronze Bars. Keep coal in the tank, because every bar needs one.
  • Silver from 150 m and gold from 300 m sell for much more than iron. Topaz and emerald are small but very valuable, and easier to find on the gem side to the right.
  • The Pulley Lift (600 coins and 4 Iron Bars) saves a lot of climbing once your shaft is deep.
  • Granite is a long stretch. Use the time to fill museum shelves and save up for the Heavy Rock Pick.

Late game: Basalt and beyond

  • The Crystal Pick needs Gold Bars and platinum, and the Magma Pick needs cobalt and diamonds. Platinum and cobalt come easier out west and diamonds out east, so the deep tools send you both ways.
  • The Elevator and the Pneumatic Tube make the long trips home quick.
  • Deep down, coins pile up fast. Spend them on meals before long digs and on the big tanks, whose Sell-all bonus pays you back.
  • Veins deep down are worth following all the way to their ends.
  • Below 500 m there is no coal in the ground. Build the Charcoal Kiln before you need it, and keep some coal for smelting and for your Miner Bot.
  • Once you pass 300 m, build the Bot Garage and park your bot on a rich layer above you while you push deeper.

Tips

  • Top up at the Cook's kitchen before every trip, and carry a flask or two when you plan a long dig to the side.
  • Take a contract before a trip so you know what to look for, and keep the minerals it needs in your tank instead of selling them.
  • Watch the tank gauge: if it's red, sell before your next trip or part of your pack won't fit.
  • Check the Equipment list before selling. The numbers in brackets show what's already in your tank.
  • When the pack is full, extra ore you mine is left behind, so head home before you crack that big gem.
  • Place a beam as soon as you hear creaking. Two beams cover a 14-tile tunnel.
  • If you're lost, press Home. Tap it again, or any direction, to stop.
  • Hold down while standing on a rope or ladder to slide down it fast. Let go to stop.
  • Before dropping into a pit or cave, check you have a rope to get back out.
  • Carry beams when you dig long tunnels to the sides: a run of 5 or more tiles can cave in.
  • Sound effects can be switched off with the Sound button at the top.
  • Want a fresh start? The ↺ button at the top starts a brand-new game after asking you first.

For parents

  • Cost: DigDeeper is free. There are no purchases, no coins for sale and no payment screens.
  • Ads: the game shows no ads.
  • Saving: progress saves automatically in the browser on your device. There's no account and no sign-in. Clearing the browser's site data deletes the save, and a save on one device doesn't move to another.
  • Online: the game loads from our website and then runs on its own. It doesn't send anything about the player anywhere. There's no chat and no contact with other players.
  • Content: a dwarf miner drawn in an old field-guide style, rocks, gems and fossils. There's no fighting. The only "danger" is a tunnel caving in, which just fills with soft rubble. Dynamite makes a cartoon bang.
  • Early test: the game is new and still changing. A big update could change how things work.

FAQ

Is DigDeeper free?

Yes. It's free to play in your browser, and there's nothing to buy with real money.

Do I need to download anything?

No. Open the game's web page on a phone, tablet or computer and tap "Start digging".

Where is my progress saved?

In your browser, on the device you play on. It saves by itself. If you clear the browser's data for the site, the save is gone.

How wide is the mine?

100 columns, with camp in the middle and bedrock walls at both ends. Metals are richer to the left, gems to the right.

How deep does it go?

There's no bottom. Magma Rock starts at 1,200 m and goes on forever, and every new 100 m record pays coins.

Why can't I dig here?

The layer is too hard for your digging pick. One layer too hard digs very slowly; two layers too hard won't dig at all. Buy the next one at Equipment.

Why can't I dig up?

Plain rock only digs down and sideways, though you can mine a mineral right above you. To go up, shoot a rope with the 🪢 button and climb it, or press Home to walk back along your ropes.

The Home button says "No way home". What now?

There's no roped way up from where you are. Shoot a rope with 🪢 and climb, or dig sideways to reach a shaft you've already roped. If you have no ropes left, pressing Home gives you a spare one.

Why does Home stop before the landing pad?

So you can keep what's in your pack. Home walks you to camp; step onto the pad yourself when you want to unload into the tank.

Why is my dwarf digging so slowly?

Check the 💧 and 🍞 bars under the pack bar. If one is empty, digging takes twice as long. Drink a flask or eat some bread, or top up at the Cook's kitchen in camp. It could also be a layer one step too hard for your digging pick.

Why did some of my loot stay in my pack?

Your tank is full. Sell at the Trader's stall, or buy a bigger tank at the Workshop, then step back on the pad.

What happens if I miss a contract's deadline?

Nothing bad. It disappears from your list and new offers keep coming. Anything you already handed over for it is gone, so deliver once you have it all if the clock is close.

How do I start over?

Tap the ↺ button at the top of the screen. The game asks first, then erases your save and starts a brand-new mine.

Does anything happen while the game is closed?

Bars in the smelter keep smelting. Nothing bad happens while you're away.

Is there sound?

Yes: scoops, crunches, pings and sparkles for every mineral. Turn them on or off with the Sound button.

Play it now

DigDeeper runs in your browser on a phone, tablet or computer. There's nothing to download, and your progress saves automatically on your device.
