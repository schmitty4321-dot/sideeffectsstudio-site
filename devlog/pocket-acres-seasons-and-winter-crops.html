What's new in Pocket Acres: seasons and winter crops

November 7, 2026 · by Dr.Dev

Pocket Acres is my drive-it-yourself farm game. You plow, plant and harvest from the tractor seat, then sell at the market or turn your crops into better goods in the city. Seasons have always been part of it: they move prices, the weather and the fish that bite. Until this update, though, you could plant any crop at any time of year. Now each crop has its own planting seasons, and winter has crops of its own.

Planting seasons

Every crop can now be planted in two of the four seasons. When you pick a crop for a plowed field, you only see the ones that fit the season you're in. Here's the calendar:

  • Spring or summer: wheat, corn, strawberries and cotton.
  • Summer or autumn: pumpkins.
  • Spring or autumn: potatoes and carrots.
  • Autumn or winter: winter wheat, cabbage and garlic.
  • Any time: hay and trees.

A crop that's already in the ground always finishes, whatever the season does. And because hay can go in any time, a field is never stuck with nothing to plant.

I made this change because a season in Pocket Acres is only seven farm days, about 14 minutes of play. That means you plant, grow and sell inside a single season, and you feel the calendar in one sitting. Planning what goes in next is now part of the fun.

A bonus for good timing

I didn't want seasons to be only a limit, so each crop also has one best season. Plant it then and the field yields 10% more. Pumpkins do best when planted in autumn, corn in summer and cabbage in winter.

Three winter crops

Winter used to be the slow part of the year. Most crops grow at half speed in the cold, so a farm could easily sit idle. Now there are three new crops to plant in autumn or winter:

  • Winter wheat is tough. It ignores the winter slowdown and grows at full speed. It sells as wheat, and wheat fetches its best price in winter.
  • Cabbage costs less to plant than garlic and sells best in winter.
  • Garlic costs more and takes longer to grow, but it sells for one of the highest prices of any crop.

Cabbage and garlic come with new recipes in the city. The Kitchen makes coleslaw, cabbage rolls and garlic noodles. The Cannery makes cabbage soup, sauerkraut and a winter stew of cabbage, potatoes and carrots. The Bakery bakes garlic bread and the Dairy whips up garlic butter. The greenhouse is still the one place where anything grows all year.

New crops and the Tailor

Earlier the same day I added four more crops: potatoes, carrots, strawberries and cotton. They bring a batch of new goods, like French fries, strawberry jam and carrot cake. Cotton is the slowest field crop, and it feeds a new city building, the Tailor, which sews scarves, mittens, tote bags, quilts and teddy bears.

Small things

New players now get a short lesson the first time they meet something: selling a harvest, the Market, the Mill, the first weeds, and the first runaway animal. The animal pens also got new fences, each one themed for the animal inside, with a gate.

What's next

Next I want to add a short screen that explains seasons the first time you see them. Until then, this post and the player guide have the full planting calendar.
