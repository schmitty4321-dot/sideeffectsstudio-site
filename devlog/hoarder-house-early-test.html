Hoarder House: one full kitchen, one golden spork

November 24, 2026 · by Dr.Dev

Hoarder House is out today as an early test. It's a cozy game about one very full house. The kitchen floor is buried under 125 things, from a stack of plates to a giant cheese wheel, and your job is to dig them out, carry each one to where it belongs, pack the drawers, and then scrub the place clean. Somewhere at the bottom is Grandma's Golden Spork, and she wants it back before Sunday soup.

It started as Coin Cavern

Five days ago I made a copy of Coin Cavern, my game about digging through a room of coins to find a lost ring, and took the coin machine out. What was left was a room, a camera you slide with one finger, and a big pile on the floor. Coin Cavern taught me how to draw a huge pile on a phone without slowing it down: only the top layers are real, the rest is just height.

In Hoarder House the pile is made of stacks of real things. You can only take the top one, and if you dig a hole next to a tall stack, the stack slides over. Every one of the 185 things in the two rooms is its own 3D model, built from a picture, and all the soft junk packed around them (bags, boxes, newspapers) can be flicked into the toss bag just because it feels good.

Drawers are the puzzle

The part I most wanted to get right is putting things away. Each drawer and shelf is a grid, and each thing has a real outline: a fork takes two squares, a frying pan is a square with a handle, a rolling pin is four long. You turn them and fit them together until the drawer locks with a cha-ching. Lock it with mostly the right things and you get a second star. Arrange it well, like a rainbow row of spices or the rarest treasure at the front of the shelf, and you get the third.

Before anyone played it, a little robot player cleared and packed the kitchen again and again to check every drawer could actually be filled. It found that the first kitchen pile was too small: some drawers could only be finished with things from rooms that don't exist yet. So the kitchen got a bigger pile, 125 things, and every drawer can now lock from the kitchen's own stuff. The robot also showed that two drawers usually couldn't lock because the waiting trays filled up too soon, so trays now hold a quarter more than their drawers.

Small fixes from testing

  • I dragged the basil to the spice rack and nothing seemed to happen. It was waiting in the rack's tray to be packed. Now every piece with things waiting shows a 📥 badge, and the first drop explains it.
  • Some drawers were full but things were still waiting. Now the Donate button pulses and says so, and a To floor button lets you carry an extra somewhere else.
  • On launch day I made every piece turn freely and switched off gravity on shelves. Both made packing easier and more fun.

What's in the test

Two rooms: the kitchen (11 pieces of furniture, 22 drawers and shelves to fill) and the pantry (60 things, 7 spaces, and a Golden Pineapple that Dad hid as a joke). There's a short tutorial, a carry basket for things that belong in other rooms, five cleaning tools, a before-and-after picture for the kitchen, and a grade from A+ to F for every room. It's free, with no ads, no time limit and nothing to buy. The furniture is still simple stand-in art.

What I'd love to hear

Nobody has timed a room on a real phone yet. My robot thinks a kitchen takes about 15 minutes to clear and pack and 5 more to clean, so I'd love to know how long it takes you. I'd also like to know if pressing on the pile to grab things gets in the way of moving the view, whether three Auto-fits a room feels right, and whether the bonus goals on each drawer make sense. If it's slow on your phone, tell me which phone through the contact page.

What's next

On my list: a before-and-after picture for the pantry, things in the carry basket turning up in their own rooms, real furniture in place of the stand-ins, and then the rest of the house, room by room, starting with the dining room. None of that has a date yet. If you play, I'd love to know which drawer gave you the most trouble.
