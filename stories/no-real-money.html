What “no real money, ever” means

November 10, 2026 · by Dr.Dev

Every game page on this site says the same thing: free to play, no real money, ever. It's an easy line to write and a harder one to keep, so here is exactly what it means, what it doesn't mean, and why I made it a rule for the whole studio instead of a choice for each game.

What it means

  • There is no store. None of our games has a shop that takes a card, a gift card, a phone bill payment or an app store purchase. There is no button anywhere that turns real money into anything.
  • Every coin is earned by playing. Coins, credits, gems, gold and every other currency in our games come from playing: filling orders, finishing raids, selling a harvest, running a caravan route. You can't buy more of them, and you can't buy the things they buy.
  • Nothing is locked behind a payment. Every character, level, map and upgrade can be reached by playing. Some take a while. That's the game, not a sales funnel.
  • No "premium" version. There is no paid edition with the good parts in it. The free game is the whole game.

Why it's a rule

Kids play these games. Plenty of adults do too, often on a short break, and I want that break to stay a break. Free games that sell currency are built around one moment: the point where waiting feels worse than paying. Once a game has a store, every timer, every price and every reward has a second job, which is to nudge you toward it. I would rather design every one of those numbers for one purpose only: making the game fun to play.

It also makes a parent's job simple. You don't need to check settings, set up a password for purchases or read the fine print of each game. There's nothing to buy, so there's nothing to block.

What about packs, spins and boxes?

Some of our games have surprise rewards: a crew pack in Brainrot Gem Quest, a Boss Box in Tap Tap Sahur, a prize wheel in Hatch & Grow. They're paid for only with things you earned in the game, and they can never be bought with real money. Nothing you win can be cashed out or traded for money either. They are part of the fun of collecting, not a way to sell you a chance at something.

So how are the games paid for?

Honestly: mostly by my spare time, plus small hosting costs. To keep the games free over the long run, this website and the games may show ads from Google. Here's how I'm approaching that:

  • Optional first. The ads I want most are the kind you choose, like "watch an ad for a bonus". Skip it and you lose nothing you already had.
  • Not in the middle of play. No ads that pop up while you're in the middle of a round.
  • Safe for kids. Ads here are set to Google's treatment for child-directed content, which means non-personalized ads only. The details are in our privacy policy.

The other promises that go with it

"No real money" sits next to three other rules that every game here follows:

  • No accounts. You never sign up, and no game asks for your name or email. Your progress is saved in your own browser.
  • No chat with strangers. There's no messaging between players in any of our games. Where players meet, like the Egg Rush leaderboard in Hatch & Grow or the battles in Brainrot Gem Quest, you only see made-up nicknames and scores.
  • Free, start to finish. The whole game, for everyone.

If you ever find something in one of our games that looks like it breaks these rules, tell me. I'll treat it as a bug.
