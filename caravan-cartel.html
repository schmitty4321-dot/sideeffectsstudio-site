Caravan Cartel

A desert trading game. Lead camel caravans between towns, buy cheap where a good is made, sell dear where it's wanted, and decide on every trip how much water to carry instead of cargo.

How to play

  1. Pick a town. Tap a town on the map to open its market. Each good is either made here (cheap to buy) or wanted (pays well when you sell).
  2. Send a caravan. Choose a destination under "Go to". The button shows the walking time, the stops on the way, and a 🔥 if the trail crosses hot ground.
  3. Buy low, sell high. Buy fills your free cargo space. Sell sells everything of that good. Every unit you trade moves the price a little.
  4. Carry enough water. Each camel carries barrels as well as goods. More water opens longer, faster trails but leaves less room for cargo.
  5. Let it run itself. After a round trip, tap your caravan and press Repeat this trip. It now trades that loop on its own, even while the game is closed.
  6. Grow. Buy more camels and caravans, send scouts into the fog, buy upgrades and outposts, and climb the 10 Guild ranks.

The four tabs at the bottom are the Map, Caravans (all your caravans and scouts, and the button to start a new caravan), the Ledger (the best deal you know for each good) and the Guild (ranks, contracts, upgrades, relics). Your gold and your next rank goal sit at the top.

Your first 10 minutes

You start in Wellspring, your Home Base, with 20 gold, one camel named Pip carrying 6 dates, and a scout called Nell. You already know three places: Wellspring, the salt town of Saltby, and the well at Brine Spring between them. Everything else is fog with ❓ markers on the trails.

A short tutorial walks you through your first trade, one step at a time, with a tip box at the top of the screen. Caravans and scouts move extra fast while it runs, so the first trips take seconds.

  1. Tap Saltby and send Pip there. Saltby wants dates, so sell them.
  2. Salt is made in Saltby, so it's cheap. Buy salt.
  3. Send Pip home. Wellspring wants salt, so sell it there, then buy dates for the next trip.
  4. Tap Pip and press Repeat this trip. Pip now runs Wellspring to Saltby and back by himself.
  5. Tap a ❓ in the fog to send Nell scouting. The first scouting trip is free.
  6. Buy Pip a second camel (the first extra camel costs 40 gold) so he carries more.

When the tutorial ends, a message points you to the Guild tab. From then on you'll also see contracts and events on the trail, which are explained below. Your first Guild goal is to sell 150 gold of goods; the dates and salt run gets you there quickly.

Water and cargo: the big choice

Every camel carries 10 weight. Water barrels are part of that weight: at the start each barrel weighs 1, so a camel with 2 barrels has room for 8 weight of goods. The Light barrels upgrade makes barrels weigh 0.75, then 0.5.

Barrels are counted per camel. Each trail between two places drinks a set number of barrels from every camel, and a caravan can only take trails its barrels cover. Water refills for free at every town, oasis and well, so a long trip broken up by wells needs fewer barrels at a time.

The start area is built around this choice:

  • Wellspring to Saltby: the Salt Flats shortcut needs 3 barrels per camel. The safe way goes through Brine Spring and needs 1, then 2, but takes longer.
  • Wellspring to Palm Rest: the Red Hardpan shortcut needs 3. The safe way through the Thornwell well needs 1, then 2.

Trails marked 🔥 cross hot ground. On each hot leg there's a 20% chance the heat makes every camel drink one extra barrel. If a caravan runs dry it walks much slower and drops about 15% of each good it carries. When more water would open a quicker trail, the town panel shows a ⚡ hint with buttons like "Carry 3 barrels" and "Carry 4 (safe)". You can change barrels any time from the caravan's panel; on the trail, new barrels fill at the next water stop.

The shortcut, the safe shortcut and the detour all earn about the same on purpose. The safe choice, one spare barrel on hot trails, is the most reliable, and spare water also helps with the Lost traveler event.

Goods and towns

There are 8 goods. Heavier and rarer goods are worth more, so compare them by profit per weight, which is what the Ledger shows.

  • 🌴 Dates (weight 1): made in Wellspring. Wanted in Saltby, Palm Rest and Copperhill.
  • 🧂 Salt (weight 1): made in Saltby. Wanted in Wellspring, Loomstead and Glassreach.
  • 🌵 Cactus jam (weight 1): made in Palm Rest. Wanted in Copperhill, Loomstead and Tidewind.
  • 🟠 Copper (weight 3): made in Copperhill. Wanted in Saltby, Glassreach and Ambergate.
  • 🧶 Woven rugs (weight 2): made in Loomstead. Wanted in Wellspring, Glassreach and Tidewind.
  • 🔮 Sand glass (weight 2): made in Glassreach. Wanted in Copperhill and Ambergate.
  • 🕯️ Incense (weight 1): made in Ambergate. Wanted in Wellspring and Tidewind.
  • 🦪 Sunpearls (weight 1): made in Tidewind. Wanted in Saltby and Glassreach.

A good stays hidden until you know a town that makes it. Prices are live only where one of your caravans is standing. Everywhere else you see the last price your caravans saw, with how old it is.

Markets react to you. Each unit you sell pushes that town's price down, and each unit you buy pushes it up. Towns that make a good keep big stocks, so their prices move slowly. Towns that want a good keep small stocks, so selling a big load there drops the price fast. Stocks recover about half of the way back every minute, and every price also drifts slowly up and down over time.

Not every place is a market. Wells (Brine Spring, Thornwell, Old Well) and the oasis of Mirage Pool give water but have no shop. Ruins and landmarks can only be reached by scouts, and finding them pays off: Sunken Gate holds 120 gold and the Old Compass (scouts 30% faster), Cracked Dome holds 300 gold and the Copper Bell (goods sell for 5% more), and the Buried Bazaar holds 2,500 gold and the Star Map (offline earnings +15%). The Stone Arch landmark makes caravans walk 10% faster, and the Sky Needle adds 15% to offline earnings.

Strategy

Early game: Wellspring and Saltby

  • Keep Pip on the dates and salt loop. Dates sell for about twice in Saltby what they cost in Wellspring, and salt does the same the other way, so the loop earns in both directions.
  • Spend early gold on camels first. A bigger caravan carries more on the same trip. Camel prices rise by about a third each time.
  • Saddlebags (150 gold) adds 2 carry weight to every camel you own, and it's a good second buy.
  • Scout the nearby fog: Thornwell opens the safe way to Palm Rest, and Old Well links Saltby to Palm Rest and Loomstead. Scouting costs a little more for each place you know, and twice as much next to the Dune Sea, so scout where you plan to trade.
  • A second caravan costs 100 gold and starts at Wellspring. Rank 4 asks you to run 2 caravans on routes.

Middle game: Copperhill, Loomstead and Glassreach

  • Palm Rest's cactus jam sells well in Copperhill and Loomstead, and Copperhill wants dates and jam too, so Wellspring, Palm Rest and Copperhill make a strong loop.
  • Copper is heavy (weight 3). It pays, but check the Ledger's profit per weight before filling a caravan with it.
  • Loomstead's rugs are wanted back home in Wellspring. The trail from Loomstead to Glassreach drinks 3 barrels per camel, so plan water before you head north.
  • Use Plan a route by hand to build longer loops with several stops. Tap a good at each stop to cycle Sell, Buy and off. Sells happen before buys, and a route skips a buy when the last price it saw at the next selling stop is no better, so it won't trade at a loss on purpose.
  • Take contracts from the Guild tab. A contract asks you to sell a number of one good at a town within 10, 15 or 20 minutes, and pays a bonus on top of the market price. Sales by any of your caravans count. You can hold 2 at a time, and offers you don't take leave after 10 minutes.
  • The Warehouse at Wellspring stores goods (40 weight at first, up to 1,500). A route that visits Wellspring delivers goods it buys but never sells, which lets one caravan stock up for another.

Late game: the Dune Sea

  • North of Glassreach lies the Dune Sea. Every trail there is hot and drinks twice the usual water: Glassreach to Mirage Pool needs 3 barrels per camel, Mirage Pool to Ambergate needs 4, and Mirage Pool to Tidewind needs 5.
  • Outposts can be built at any oasis or well you know. The first costs 800 gold and each one after that costs double. An outpost has storage, a cistern that cuts the water needed on every trail leaving it by 1 barrel per camel, and a pigeon loft that gives you live prices in towns one trail away. An outpost at Mirage Pool makes the Dune Sea far easier.
  • Incense from Ambergate and Sunpearls from Tidewind are the most valuable goods by weight. Incense sells back in Wellspring and Tidewind; Sunpearls sell in Saltby and Glassreach.
  • The Caravanserai raises how long your caravans keep earning while you're away, from 2 hours to 4, then 8.
  • The last ranks ask you to discover every place, then own 6 caravans and sell 1,000,000 gold of goods.

Events on the trail

After the tutorial, each leg of a trip has a 10% chance of an event. A caravan you're steering by hand shows a choice card. A caravan on a route always takes the safe choice by itself, and so does any card you leave unanswered.

  • Sandstorm: wait it out (slower) or push through for 1 barrel per camel. Pushing through with no water drops some cargo.
  • Passing merchant: offers to swap your biggest stack for a better-valued good you know.
  • Lost traveler: share 1 spare barrel per camel for a gold reward, and sometimes directions to a place in the fog.
  • Dust foxes: shoo them (a little slower) or let them run off with 1 to 3 of one good.

Tips

  • Sell a big load across two towns that want it instead of dumping it all in one.
  • Open the Ledger before you plan a route. Old prices may have changed, so check their age.
  • Only caravans on routes earn while you're away, at 70% of their measured rate. Put every caravan on a route before you close the game.
  • If a route breaks, the caravan didn't have enough water for a leg. Add barrels and make a new route.
  • Scouts discover a place halfway through their trip, then walk home.
  • Music can be switched off with the ⚙ button at the top.

For parents

  • Cost: Caravan Cartel is free. There are no purchases, no coins for sale and no payment screens.
  • Ads: the game shows no ads. The welcome-back screen has a "double it" button marked as a mock; it doubles the offline gold and plays nothing.
  • Saving: progress saves automatically in the browser on your device. There's no account and no sign-in. Clearing the browser's site data deletes the save, and a save on one device doesn't move to another.
  • Online: the game loads from our website, and the music file loads after the first tap. The game doesn't send anything about the player anywhere. There's no chat and no contact with other players.
  • Content: camels, markets and maps. There's no fighting, and the only "danger" is running out of water or foxes stealing a few dates. Reading prices and comparing numbers is part of the fun, so younger children may want help at first.

FAQ

Is Caravan Cartel free?

Yes. It's free to play in your browser, and there's nothing to buy with real money.

Do I need to download anything?

No. Open the game's web page on a phone, tablet or computer and tap "Begin your journey".

Where is my progress saved?

In your browser, on the device you play on. It saves by itself. If you clear the browser's data for the site, the save is gone.

Do my caravans earn while the game is closed?

Yes, if they're on a route. They earn 70% of the gold per second they were making, for up to 2 hours (4 or 8 hours with the Caravanserai upgrade). Scouts also finish their trips. Nothing bad happens to your caravans while you're away.

Why can't my caravan reach a town?

One of the trails needs more water barrels per camel than the caravan carries. The town panel says how many barrels it needs. Add barrels in the caravan's panel, or go the long way through a well.

Why did my caravan slow down and lose goods?

It ran out of water. Hot trails (🔥) sometimes drink an extra barrel. Carry one spare barrel per camel on hot trails to be safe.

Why are some prices shown as "?" or old?

You only see live prices where a caravan is standing, or next to an outpost's pigeon loft. Elsewhere you see the last price your caravans saw.

How do I find new towns?

Tap a ❓ in the fog and send a scout. Buy more scouts with the Scout tent upgrade in the Guild tab.

Is there sound?

There's a looping desert music track. Turn it on or off with the ⚙ button.
