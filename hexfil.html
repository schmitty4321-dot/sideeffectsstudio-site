HEXFIL

Sneak in. Loot. Get out before your deck runs out. HEXFIL is a turn-based extraction raid on a hex map: slip into an enemy camp, crack its crates and safes, fight guards in card duels, and reach an exit while you still have cards to play.

Early test: HEXFIL went live on November 24, 2026, and it's still being built. One map is open, and the numbers will change after people play it. Nobody outside the studio has played a full raid on a phone yet, so your feedback really counts.

How to play

New player? Your very first raid is a guided training raid: a coach walks you through walking, searching, sight cones, an ambush, a card fight and getting out, one step at a time. Nothing can be lost in it, and you can replay it any time from the Info room.

  1. Pick a way in at home base: on foot (free, but the walk costs a card), by truck or by helicopter. Choose day or night and your loadout, then tap START RAID.
  2. Move and act with cards. Each turn you hold 4 cards and have 3 action points (AP). Walking is always there on the boots card at the left of your hand.
  3. Search crates, safes and bodies for loot. The better the find, the longer its ring climbs from grey to gold.
  4. Stay out of sight, or fight: guards that spot you start a card fight.
  5. Get out at an exit before your deck runs out. Everything in your bag is sold for cash when you get home.

Your turn

  • 3 AP a turn. Most cards cost 1 AP; heavy cards cost 2 (they show 2▶). Searching costs 2 AP and any card.
  • The boots card is always there: tap it once to Walk (1 AP, 2 hexes, guards next to you hear it), tap again to Run (2 AP, 3 hexes, heard 4 hexes away). Crouch and Slide are silent moves you draw.
  • Your hand tops back up to 4 at the start of each turn. You can discard any card for free (a Burden card costs 1 AP to heave off).
  • The deck is the clock. When the draw pile is empty you lose 3 HP a turn, so leave while you still have cards.
  • Heavy loot (🎒) puts a dead Burden card into your deck. Worth it for a gold bar, maybe not for a car battery.

Guards and the Alarm

Every guard has a red sight cone. At the end of your turn, a guard that sees you comes to check (?), and a second look starts a fight (!). Get behind one for a silent takedown. Noise matters too: running, gunfire and explosions bring guards over. The Alarm climbs from 0 to 3 as you're spotted and shooting starts; at 2 a hunter comes looking, and at 3 the main gate locks.

Card fights

A fight is one card each, back and forth. Before you choose, the enemy shows its next move (shoot, take aim, take cover, flank, throw a grenade) and a line under it lists every card left in its deck, so you can plan around what's coming. Snap Shot and Burst fire first, Aimed Shot fires after it at +20%, Crouch and Slide make it harder to hit you, and Smoke gets you out. Each exchange draws you a card, so long fights eat your clock. Ambush a guard that hasn't seen you for a free opening shot.

Loot, locks and the HQ

  • Loot comes in five tiers, from junk to legendary. Medical and supply finds also give you their card.
  • Locks: use a key you found, pick it (play cards whose 🔑 number matches each pin), or force it with bolt cutters, a crowbar or a breach charge.
  • Terminals: with a hack rig, play cards in the right colour order to drop the Alarm, open the armory or cut the radio.
  • The HQ has a ground floor, an upstairs and a basement. Rooms stay dark until you open their door. The basement vault opens with the vault code, or by itself on turn 12.
  • Bosses: the Quartermaster, Warden Ironjaw, the Radio Man and Chef Cleaver. One is in every raid, and they all carry something special.
  • A rival crew raids the same camp. It loots crates, fights guards and heads for the exits. Beat it and its whole bag is yours.

Getting out

There are three exits: the main gate (closed at Alarm 3), the creek bridge, and the helipad, where you wait two turns while your flare draws attention. Die, and you lose the gear you wore and your uninsured guns, but your Secure Pouch item comes home. Your body stays where you fell, so the next raid can be a corpse run to get it back.

Home base

  • Operator: every raid earns XP. Levels give stat points (Aim, Grit, Stealth, Tech, Dexterity, Carry, Nerve), skill points for a four-branch skill tree, and a trait every five levels.
  • Workbench: upgrade cards with Parts and cash, so every copy is better in every raid.
  • Gunsmith, Tailor and Quartermaster sell guns, attachments, gear and throwables, and the Tailor repairs armor plates.
  • Fence: sell spare kit for cash or scrap it for Parts.
  • Insurance on the loadout screen brings a lost gun back: cheap and slow, or pricey and quick.

The War Room

The War Room's map of Graywater Valley posts four jobs a day: loot runs, a briefcase to recover, terminals to hack, points to photograph, targets to sabotage, and a boss to take down. Two jobs pit you against another crew: in a Supply Drop Race a crate parachutes in on turn 4 and a rival crew races you for its sealed cargo, and in a Bounty you hunt a named crew that's carrying a piece of gear you lost when you went down. In a Hostage Rescue you free a prisoner and walk them out: they follow right behind you, and in a fight they can only be hit if you ran that turn or you're standing in the open, so fight from cover. In Hold the Line you switch on a radio uplink and stay next to it for 3 to 5 turns while guards come at you in waves. Jobs pay cash, XP and Fixer reputation, sometimes kit too, and they expire after a few days. Each raid is a day. Loud raids raise the camp's Heat, which adds patrols but makes jobs pay more. A good job can lead to a better-paid follow-up.

Tips

  • Walk is quiet enough most of the time; save Run for a dash to the exit.
  • Read the enemy's next move before you play a card. Crouch against Shoot, and a move card against an aimed shot or a grenade.
  • Night raids hide you better, but without night vision you see less too.
  • Put your best small find in the Secure Pouch early.
  • Tap a card once to see it big; it shrinks to the corner after a moment so you can see the map.

For parents

  • Cost: HEXFIL is free. There are no purchases and no payment screens.
  • Ads: the game shows no ads.
  • Tone: a military stealth game with cartoon-painted guns and card fights. Defeated guards fall down and leave a body to search; there's no blood or gore. Best for teens.
  • Saving: progress saves in the browser on your device. There's no account, no chat and no contact with other players.
  • Online: everything loads from our website, about 8 MB on the first visit. The game sends nothing about the player anywhere.
  • Early test: the game is new and still changing. A small test menu (the cog) is still in the game.

FAQ

Is HEXFIL free?

Yes. It's free to play in your browser, and there's nothing to buy.

Do I need to download anything?

No. Open the game's web page on a phone, tablet or computer. It's built for an upright phone screen.

Where is my progress saved?

In your browser, on the device you play on. Clearing the browser's data for the site deletes it, and a save doesn't move between devices.

I ran out of cards. What now?

You can still walk with the boots card, but you lose 3 HP each turn. Head straight for the nearest exit.

Why did I lose my gun?

Dying loses the guns you carried unless they were insured. With no gun left, the Fixer lends you a free pistol and knife so you can always raid.

What's next?

More of Graywater Valley: a port, a refinery, a town and a mountain outpost, plus more job types. None of it has a date yet.
