HEXFIL: your deck is the clock

November 24, 2026 · by Dr.Dev

HEXFIL is out today as an early test. It's an extraction raid you play one turn at a time on a hex map: you slip into an enemy camp, loot what you can, and get out. The twist is that everything you do comes from a deck of cards, and that deck is your clock. When it runs out, you start bleeding, so every search and every fight is a choice between one more crate and getting home.

Cards instead of a timer

Extraction games usually run on a clock or a shrinking zone. I wanted the pressure to come from your own hand instead. Each turn you hold four cards and have three action points. Moving, searching, picking a lock and shooting all spend cards, and the deck only refills once. The longer you stay, the thinner your options get.

The walking problem

The first version put Walk and Sprint cards in the deck, and the test robot kept getting stuck with a hand full of guns and nowhere to go. So walking moved onto a card that's always beside your hand: tap it to walk, tap again to run, and it costs action points instead of cards. Crouch and Slide stayed in the deck as the silent moves you hope to draw. Then raids got too easy, so searching now costs two action points. Every turn you either loot or cover ground.

Fights you can read

When a guard spots you, the map pauses for a card fight, one card each. You always see the enemy's next move, and now you can also see every card left in its deck, like counting cards in a board game. A grunt's deck has four Shoots, an Aim, a Take cover, a Suppressing fire, a Flank and a Reload. Bosses have nastier decks, and switch to a new one at half health.

What's in the test

One enemy camp with a three-floor HQ, four bosses, a rival crew, locks, hacking, gas, night raids, and a home base with gear, insurance, a skill tree, card upgrades and a War Room that posts daily jobs. It's free, with no ads and nothing to buy. The art is painted, but some icons are still stand-ins.

What I'd love to hear

Nobody outside the studio has played a full raid on a phone yet. I'd love to know how many turns your raids take, whether you run out of cards or out of health first, and whether searching for two action points feels right. Tell me through the contact page.

What's next

More of Graywater Valley: a port, a refinery, a town and a mountain outpost, plus hostage rescues and bounty hunts against rival crews. None of it has a date yet.
