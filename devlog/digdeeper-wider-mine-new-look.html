DigDeeper: a wider mine, hold to mine and a new look

November 13, 2026 · by Dr.Dev

DigDeeper came out as an early test yesterday, and the first round of playing it turned up three things I wanted to change straight away: the mine felt narrow, tapping every rock got tiring, and the art didn't feel like a real expedition yet. All three are fixed in today's update.

A mine 100 columns wide

Until now the mine was exactly one screen wide. Now it's 100 columns, with your camp in the middle. Walk or dig toward the edge of the screen and it glides along with you. Solid bedrock walls mark the two ends.

I didn't want the extra ground to be more of the same, so each side has its own riches. The same layers run all the way across, but to the left is metal country, where copper, iron, silver, gold, platinum and cobalt are far more common, and to the right is gem country, where quartz, amethyst, topaz, emerald, sapphire, ruby and diamond are. The deep tools need both, so you end up working both sides.

The catch is the walk home. A rich seam 40 columns out is a long trip with a full pack, so a small sign at the screen edge always points back to camp, and the Home button now works from anywhere, even out on the grass. I checked the numbers with the same robot that plays every game first: digging under camp earns exactly what it did before, and the sides pay a little more per rock to make up for the trip.

Hold to mine

You no longer have to tap every rock. Keep holding toward a mineral and your dwarf swings the pick until it pops, then steps in if you keep holding. Harder minerals take more swings, just like they took more taps, and a better pick still makes it quicker. It works up, too, so you can mine a mineral right above your head. Tapping still works if you prefer it.

A new look

The whole game now looks like an old field guide: engraved rock layers, illustrated minerals, timber ladders and supports, a sepia surface camp, and a pickaxe that actually strikes the rock with chips flying off. The first time you find each mineral, a specimen card from your field journal pops up with a short note about it. You can also slide down a ladder by holding down on it.

All that art made the game heavy, so I shrank it before release: it now loads about 2 MB instead of nearly 12.

Also new

  • A ↺ button at the top starts a brand-new game, after asking first.
  • The Home button no longer gets confused by a one-tile hole in a tunnel floor.
  • The shop is now called Equipment, and the camp is the Field Camp.

Your old save still works, and the mine under your camp is exactly where you left it. If you play, I'd love to know which side you dig first.
