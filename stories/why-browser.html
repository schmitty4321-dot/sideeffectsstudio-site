Why every game plays in your browser

November 10, 2026 · by Dr.Dev

None of our games are in an app store. You tap a link, and a few seconds later you're playing. That's a deliberate choice, and like most choices it has an upside and a catch. Here are both.

The upside

  • Nothing to install. No download, no storage space, no permissions screen. It works on an iPhone, an Android phone, a tablet, a Chromebook or a computer.
  • No account, no store. App stores want accounts and payment details on file. A browser game needs neither, which fits our no-real-money rule.
  • Updates just happen. When I fix a bug or add something, the next time you open the game you have it. There's no update to wait for.
  • Easy to share. A game is a link. Send it to a friend and they're playing in seconds.

The catch: your save lives in your browser

Without accounts, there's nowhere on a server to keep your progress. Instead, each game saves to your browser's own storage, on your own device. That's private and instant, but it comes with three things worth knowing:

  • Clearing site data erases it. If you clear your browser's history or "cookies and site data", or a phone cleaner app does it for you, your save goes with it.
  • Each browser has its own save. Progress in Safari doesn't show up in Chrome, and progress on your phone doesn't follow you to your laptop.
  • Each address has its own save. I learned this one the hard way. Our games used to live at other web addresses before they moved to their homes on sideeffectsstudio.buzz. Because a save belongs to an address, players who had been playing at the old addresses started fresh at the new ones. I'm sorry if that was you. The addresses linked from this site are the permanent ones.

How to keep your progress safe

  • Play in your normal browser window, not a private or incognito one. Private windows throw away their storage when you close them.
  • Add the game to your home screen. On iPhone: tap Share, then "Add to Home Screen". On Android: open the browser menu and tap "Add to Home screen" or "Install". It opens like an app and keeps the same save.
  • Stick to one device and one browser for each game.

Will there ever be app versions?

Maybe, one day, for a game that really needs it. For now, the browser lets me make more games, update them faster and keep every one of them free with no store in the way. If a game you love would be better as an app, tell me which one and why.
