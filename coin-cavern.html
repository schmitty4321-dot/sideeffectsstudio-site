Coin Cavern

A room 10 feet deep in coins, and one lost thing somewhere inside it. Scoop the coins, feed them to a sorting machine with far too many moving parts, spend what it earns on better tools, and keep going until you find it.

How to play

  1. Take the job. Each room starts with a client from Lost & Found Co. who tells you what they lost. The card lists its three details, like "Gold band, Blue stone, Engraved Always".
  2. Scoop. Tap the coin pile to scoop coins into your bin. Hold your finger down to keep scooping.
  3. Haul. Drag your full bin up onto the hopper, the funnel at the top of the machine, and let go. It pours itself in.
  4. Sort and sell. Each coin rides the machine for 8 seconds and sells when it lands in its clear collector.
  5. Upgrade. Open the Shop and buy bigger scoops, bigger bins, a faster machine and helpers.
  6. Find it. Keep digging. The lost item turns up near the bottom of the room, and you'll know the moment you scoop it.

The top of the screen shows your money, the clock, coins left and the Wardrobe, Collectors, Room and Book buttons. The bottom panel shows your bin, the hopper and the Shop button.

Coins come in four kinds, all mixed together: pennies ($10, half of the pile), silver coins ($50, a quarter), blue coins ($100) and gold coins ($250). Gold coins are only 10% of the coins but about 43% of the money. The dollars are pretend, and they only buy things inside the game.

Your first 10 minutes

Your first room is Grandma's Coin Closet. Grandma June's wedding ring is somewhere in fifty years of Walt's saved coins: a gold band with a blue stone, engraved "Always". Tap Take the job, look over the room survey, and tap Enter the room. The clock starts when you enter.

A 7-step tutorial walks you through the loop with a card and a pointer. You can skip it at any step, and replay it later from the Room screen.

  1. Dig in. You start with your bare hands, 5 coins a scoop, and a small tub that holds 40.
  2. Fill your bin. Eight handfuls fill it.
  3. To the machine. Drag the tub onto the hopper. If you let go past halfway it docks and dumps. Otherwise it slides back home.
  4. Watch it sort. Coins sell as they land, so your money ticks up about 8 seconds after you pour.
  5. Spend it. Open the Shop.
  6. Get a bigger scoop. The Coffee mug costs $6,000 and scoops 25 coins instead of 5. It takes about three tubs of coins to afford.
  7. Now find the lost item. Tap Room any time to see what you're looking for.

Around your first 700 coins, the penny collector gets close to full. That's your cue to buy the coin wrapper for $8,000. More on that below.

Digging and hauling

You can only scoop while your bin is in the room. Tools go from your hands (5 coins a scoop) to the Coffee mug (25 coins, $6,000), the Sand bucket (120, $40,000) and the Shovel (400, $95,000). Your bin goes from the Small tub (40 coins) to the Big tub (150, $6,000), the Barrel (600, $60,000) and the Dumpster cart (2,000, $350,000). A bigger bin also pours faster. If the hopper fills up, the bin waits over it instead of spilling. Hauling upgrades take the walking out:

  • Pulley line ($25,000): tap the bin, or the Send bin button, and it rides a cable to the hopper, dumps, and comes back. A full bin goes by itself.
  • Second bin ($100,000): two buckets on the pulley. While one pours, the other is back in the room for you to fill.
  • Conveyor chute ($400,000): no more bin trips. Every scoop drops into a funnel and rides a belt to the hopper in 2 seconds.

Slide with one finger to look around, and pinch (or use the mouse wheel) to zoom. The view buttons jump to the Vault, the Coin pile or the Sorter, and Free cam lets you spin the camera.

The sorting machine

Every coin takes the long way. In the vault machine (Rooms 3 to 10), coins leave the hopper on a feeder wheel, roll down zig-zag rails past a weigh scale and polish brushes, loop through a glass loop-the-loop, ride a bucket elevator, bounce through a plinko board and past a magnet drum that picks out the blue coins, then drop into their clear collectors. Each trip takes 8 seconds, and a coin is paid for when it lands.

The first two rooms have their own machines. Grandma's Coin Closet has the Sewing Room Sorter, with tape-measure rails, a thimble lift and a music box where June and Walt dance every 1,000th coin. The Penny Parlor has the Spoon Cascade: seesaw spoons, a Ferris wheel of ice-cream cones, a corkscrew around a gumball globe, and a chain reaction of wafer dominoes every 500 coins. Rules and prices are the same everywhere. Only the look and the names change, so in Room 1 the faster sorters are the Foot treadle, the Sewing motor and the Overlocker.

Collectors overflow. Each collector holds 360 coins. Coins that land in a full one spill onto the floor and pay nothing. You can empty collectors by hand in the Collectors panel (money already earned stays). After three spilling pours in a row a dwarf sweeps the floor, but without the dustpan those coins are still lost.

The coin wrapper takes a full roll of 50 coins of one kind out of a collector and pays a 5% bonus on it. Rolls go into a crate that holds 12. When the crate is full the wrapper stops, so empty it in the Collectors panel, buy a bigger crate, or buy the Roll cart, which hauls 8 rolls at a time to the bank.

The shop

The shop has four tabs. Each row shows only the next thing on that line, with its price. A green dot marks a tab with something you can afford.

  • Scoopers: digging tool, bin and hauling (prices above).
  • Machine parts: Hopper (60 coins, then 250 for $10,000, 1,000 for $80,000, 4,000 for $400,000); Sorter (15 coins a second, then 50 for $16,000, 150 for $120,000, 500 for $600,000); Coin wrapper ($8,000, Twin-ram $90,000, Bank wrapper $450,000); Roll storage (24 rolls for $20,000, 36 for $60,000, no limit for $200,000); Polish (+10% per coin for $105,000, +20% for $300,000).
  • Workers: the Apprentice ($15,000), Journeyman ($200,000) and Foreman ($900,000) dig a mug, bucket or shovel full every 2 seconds into your bin. The Dustpan ($8,000) sends the sweeping dwarf as soon as coins hit the floor and tips them back into the hopper.
  • Bank: the Roll cart ($110,000) adds 5% per roll; the Bank account ($150,000) pays 2% a minute on everything banked in that room.

You can also spend money in the Wardrobe, where you dress your digger. Most looks are free; seven cost $25,000 to $250,000, from the Hi-Vis Vest to the Cardboard Bobblehead.

Odd items and the photo book

Every room hides six odd items besides the lost one: three common, two uncommon and one rare. They ride the machine like coins and roll into the tray on the left. When one arrives, the game pauses and shows its card with a finder's bonus: about $2,000 for a common item, $5,000 to $8,000 for an uncommon one, and $15,000 to $30,000 for a rare one. Tap Add to photo book (or press Enter on a keyboard) to keep it and carry on.

The tray shows the room's six slots from the start, dark until found. The items fit each client's story: Grandma's Coin Closet has Walt's love letter, an old photograph, a TV remote, a butterscotch candy, a silver thimble and Walt's reading glasses. A few link rooms together, like the Odd Sock from the arcade and a Skeleton Key from Uncle Morty's garage.

The Book button opens the photo book: one page per room, 60 odd items in all, and a Floors page for the floor paintings. Finding an item you already have still pays its bonus.

The pile also hides four Wardrobe items you can't buy: the Pirate Tricorn, the Crown, the Pirate Coat and the Map Tube. They come through the machine as wrapped parcels. Tap a parcel in the tray to open it and keep it.

Finding the ring

There's no x-ray and no metal detector, because nobody knows where the ring is. It always turns up somewhere in the room's last 5,000 coins, at a point picked at random when the room starts. Once the coins-left counter drops that low, your next scoop brings it up, wherever you dig. A steady player finds it in about 15 to 20 minutes.

Only your own scoop can find it, never your apprentice's.

Finding it is a moment: the world slows down, fireworks go off, the ring spins to face you, and a rubber stamp slams down MISSION ACCOMPLISHED. Then the coins drain away to show the painting on the floor, and the client's thank-you card shows your time and stats. After the first time, "Tap to skip" jumps to the stamp.

Look-alikes. Each room also hides 9 fakes that differ from the real thing in one or more details: a brass band instead of gold, a red stone instead of blue, "Forever" instead of "Always". They ride the machine, and when one lands a sad trombone plays and the stamp prints FAKE! with the reason, like "it has a brass band, not a gold band". Tap Keep digging to carry on. Fakes cost you nothing but a moment of hope.

Rooms

There are 10 rooms, each with a client and something different to find. Only the first is a ring. In order:

  1. Grandma's Coin Closet: June's wedding ring.
  2. The Penny Parlor: Bea Butterfield's first dollar.
  3. The Wishing Well Cellar: eight-year-old Pip's lucky marble.
  4. The Arcade Back Room: Glo's Golden Token.
  5. The Laundromat Vault: Lionel's lucky sock.
  6. Uncle Morty's Garage: Morty's car keys.
  7. The Toll Booth Attic: Gladys's retirement pin.
  8. The Fountain Basement: the nose off the town statue, Sir Splashington.
  9. The Vending Machine Graveyard: Pop Sweeney's false teeth.
  10. The Old Bank Basement: Mabel Quill's Finder No. 1 badge.

Every client card carries a postcard from someone who signs as "M.", and Room 10 reveals who that is. After room 10 the cycle starts again.

After a find, Dig a new room starts the next one from scratch: bare hands, $0, no upgrades. You keep your Wardrobe, photo book, floor paintings and best time.

Each room has a painting on the floor of the coin box, in a different style per room (Room 1's is "The wedding dance", in pastels). Once you dig down into the last foot of coins, bits of it show through the holes, and the whole painting is revealed after the find.

In the first two rooms, about a fifth of what you see in the pile isn't coins: buttons, thread spools, candies and safety pins in Grandma's closet, and bottle caps, sprinkles, gumdrops and straw bits in the Penny Parlor. They take no space and pay nothing. The machine drops them into scrap collectors, and a dwarf on a ladder carries full ones away. Buy the Scrap packer ($20,000, then $60,000 and $150,000) and a van sells the scraps in packs for $2,000 each instead.

Strategy

Early game: hands to mug

  • Buy the Coffee mug first. Five times the coins per scoop changes everything.
  • Buy the coin wrapper ($8,000) before the penny collector fills. Pennies are half of every scoop.
  • The Big tub ($6,000), Hopper II ($10,000) and Apprentice ($15,000) come next. The Dustpan ($8,000) is cheap insurance against spills.

Middle game: the pulley and the shovel

  • The Pulley line ($25,000) ends the dragging.
  • If the hopper keeps filling up, buy a faster sorter or a bigger hopper before a bigger tool. The hand-crank sorts only 15 coins a second.
  • If the roll crate keeps filling, a Big crate ($20,000) or the Roll cart ($110,000) keeps the wrapper running.

Late game: the bottom of the room

  • With the Conveyor chute and a Foreman, the pile goes down fast. Dig wherever coins are left: where the ring was buried doesn't matter.
  • Money doesn't carry to the next room, but Wardrobe items do. Once fewer than 5,000 coins are left, any scoop could end the room, so spend spare money in the Wardrobe.

Tips

  • Nothing makes the ring come before the room's last 5,000 coins, so early on, focus on money.
  • Open Collectors if coins are spilling: it shows each collector, the roll crate and what overflow has cost you.
  • The Room button shows the item's details, how many fakes are still buried and which odd items are left.
  • Start over on the Room screen wipes everything, photo book included. It asks first.

For parents

  • Cost: Coin Cavern is free. There are no purchases and no payment screens. The dollars in the game are pretend.
  • Ads: the game itself shows no ads.
  • Saving: progress saves automatically in the browser on your device. There's no account. Clearing the browser's site data deletes the save.
  • Online: there's no chat and no contact with other players. The digger's name you type in the Wardrobe stays on your device.
  • Content: coins, a sorting machine and gentle, silly clients: a grandma, an ice-cream parlour owner, a kid who wants a puppy. There's no fighting and nothing scary. Spotting fakes means reading three small details, so younger players may want help.
  • Sound: sound effects only (clinks, steam, a sad trombone for fakes), with an on/off button at the top. Screen shake in the finale is turned off if the device is set to reduce motion.
  • Time: one room takes about 15 to 20 minutes, and you can stop any time. The clock pauses while the game is closed.

FAQ

Is Coin Cavern free?

Yes. It's free to play in your browser, and there's nothing to buy with real money.

Do I need to download anything?

No. Open the game's web page on a phone, tablet or computer. It's a 3D game, so a recent device runs it best.

I've dug half the room. Why haven't I found it?

The lost item always turns up in the room's last 5,000 coins. Keep an eye on the coins-left counter. Once it's under 5,000, any scoop could be the one.

The stamp said FAKE! What happened?

That was one of the room's 9 look-alikes. The stamp tells you which detail was wrong. The real one never goes through the machine: it shows itself the moment you scoop it.

Why are coins spilling on the floor?

A collector is full (each holds 360). Buy a coin wrapper, empty the roll crate if the wrapper has stopped, or empty collectors in the Collectors panel. The Dustpan brings spilled coins back.

Why can't I scoop?

Your bin is full or away at the hopper. If the hopper is full, the bin waits there; a faster sorter or bigger hopper helps.

Why did my upgrades reset?

Every new room starts from bare hands and $0. Your Wardrobe, photo book, floor paintings and best time carry over.

Play it now

Coin Cavern runs in your browser on a phone, tablet or computer. There's nothing to download, and your progress saves automatically on your device. It's an early test, so some pictures are still being made.

Privacy

Coin Cavern keeps its save in your browser's local storage, plus two small settings (camera mode, and whether you've seen the finale). It has no accounts, ads or analytics, and doesn't send your save anywhere. Its files load from our website, and its 3D engine (three.js) loads from the public unpkg.com code library, which sees the usual request details any website sees.
