Caravan Cartel is out: water, camels and a desert tune

November 5, 2026 · by Dr.Dev

Caravan Cartel went live on November 4. It's a trading game about camels, markets and one question you answer on every trip: how much water do you carry, when every barrel takes the place of cargo? A day later I added the first music. Here's what's in the game and why it works the way it does.

One choice on every trip

I wanted a trading game where the route matters as much as the price. So every camel carries 10 weight, and water barrels count toward it. Each trail drinks a set number of barrels per camel, and a caravan can only take the trails its water covers.

I built the start area around that. Between Wellspring and Saltby there's a short shortcut across the Salt Flats that needs 3 barrels per camel, and a longer, safer way through the Brine Spring well that needs fewer at a time. Hot trails can drink an extra barrel, and a caravan that runs dry slows down and drops some of its load. I tested the lean shortcut, the safe shortcut and the detour against each other and tuned them so all three earn about the same. I didn't want one right answer. I wanted you to pick the risk you like.

What's in the launch build

The map has 17 places: towns, oases, wells, ruins and landmarks. You start knowing three. Scouts walk into the fog to find the rest, and the ruins hold gold and relics, like a Copper Bell that makes goods sell for 5% more.

Prices react to you. Every unit you sell pushes that town's price down, and the towns that want a good keep only a small stock of it, so dumping a big load in one place costs you. Spreading sales across towns pays better.

The part I'm happiest with is Repeat this trip. You trade a round trip by hand once, tap the button, and the caravan keeps running that loop on its own, even while the game is closed. Later you can plan longer routes stop by stop.

Before launch I also added outposts at oases and wells (storage, a cistern that saves water on trails leaving it, and a pigeon loft for live prices nearby), four events on the trail (sandstorms, a passing merchant, a lost traveler, dust foxes), and contracts in the Guild tab that pay a bonus for delivering a good to a town in time.

Launch day changes

On launch day I made three changes to how the game looks and feels:

  • A cut-paper title screen and a layered desert map, in place of the plain shapes I started with.
  • The camera now starts close on your first three places and pulls back slowly each time you discover something, with a gold ring on the new place. A new player isn't looking at a big empty map, and every discovery feels like the world getting bigger.
  • A new caravan panel with meters for water and cargo, the goods on board, and the route's stops in order.

The first music

On November 5 I added a 72-second desert loop, with oud, kanun, darbuka and ney. I cut the start and end so the last bar flows back into the first without a gap. It loads only after your first tap, so the title screen opens fast, and it pauses when you switch away from the tab. If you'd rather play in silence, the ⚙ button has a Music On/Off switch, and the game remembers your choice.

What's next

The game still uses simple art, and there are no sound effects yet. Ideas on my list include rival traders, a mirage event in the Dune Sea and more for your caravans to do on the long routes, but none of that is promised yet. If you play, I'd love to know which water plan you settle on.
