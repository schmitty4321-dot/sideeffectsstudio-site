Samurai Runner

An ink-wash action game for your phone. A lone ronin walks a scrolling mountain road, his weapons fire on their own, and you steer him through arrows, ninjas and walls, then hold a second finger to draw his sword in one big cut. Every road ends with a boss.

How to play

  1. Move with one thumb. Drag anywhere on the screen. The ronin follows your thumb's movement, not its position, so your thumb never has to cover him. He can roam the lower half of the road.
  2. Let your weapons fire. You never press an attack button. Your katana throws crescent waves straight ahead on its own, and so does every weapon you pick up.
  3. Dodge the red. Red is only ever used for danger: enemy arrows, knives, bones, fire, and the warning marks that appear before an attack lands. You have 5 hit points, shown as dots at the top, and a short moment of safety after each hit.
  4. Draw your sword. Hold a second finger anywhere to charge the draw cut, then lift it to cut. Lift as the ring around the ronin flashes white for a perfect cut.
  5. Grab the jade drops. Fallen foes leave small green spirit drops. They're your XP. Each level-up pauses the fight and lets you pick one of three cards.
  6. Beat the boss. When the road ends, a boss arrives. Win, and you carry coins, scrolls, spirit and jade back to your village to grow stronger for the next road.

On a computer, move with the arrow keys, WASD or by dragging with the mouse, and hold Space or the right mouse button to charge. P or Escape pauses. On a phone, the round button at the top right pauses, and switching away from the tab pauses by itself.

Your first 10 minutes

Tap Enter the village, then Set out to open the map of the Province of Rice and Ash. Only Village Outskirts is open: a road of about 1.8 minutes, then the Bandit Chief.

  1. After a few seconds a hint appears: "Hold a second finger to charge · lift to cut". Try it on a group of spearmen. Time slows while you hold.
  2. At the start you only own the katana, so your level-up cards are katana upgrades and skills like Bushidō (+10% damage).
  3. When "The road ends" appears, the Bandit Chief is coming. He sits high on the screen, so step forward to cut him, then fall back.
  4. Win or lose, the results screen shows what you earned. Falling keeps half your coins and all your scrolls and spirit, so a lost fight still counts.
  5. Back in the village, spend your coins. Unlocking the Yumi bow (60 coins) adds it to your level-up cards; a first level of Dojo training costs 40.

The draw cut

While you hold the second finger, time slows to under half speed, the ronin moves at 40% of his normal speed, and his other weapons stop firing. A ring fills around him over about 1.2 seconds. When you let go, he cuts in a sweeping arc in front of him.

  • The longer you hold, the bigger the cut. A quick tap gives a short, narrow arc. A full charge reaches past the middle of the screen and covers a half circle in front of you.
  • A strong cut clears the air. From about 85% charge, or on any perfect cut, the cut also destroys enemy arrows, bones and fire inside the arc and slices through bamboo walls.
  • Perfect cuts. The ring flashes white for a short window just after it fills. Lift inside it and the cut does double damage, the screen flashes, a 斬 seal stamps beside the ronin, and your next charge starts 35% full.
  • Don't hold forever. The cut fires by itself at twice the full charge time.

Perfect cuts are also how you earn spirit, one for each, which buys the draw-cut lessons at the Dojo. Five perfect cuts in one fight earn a star.

Weapons and level-ups

Every fight starts with only the katana at level 1. Weapons you've unlocked at the Forge can then show up as level-up cards. You can carry up to 4 weapons and 4 skills, and each goes up to level 5. You get 2 rerolls per fight to swap a hand of cards you don't like.

  • 刀 Katana wave (free): crescent waves straight ahead. Level 4 throws twin waves; level 5 waves pierce a foe. Walls stop them.
  • 弓 Yumi (60 coins): slow, hard-hitting arrows. Level 4 pierces 2 foes; level 5 looses two arrows.
  • 星 Shuriken (80 coins): a fan of 3 stars, growing to 6 stars at level 5. Good against crowds.
  • 札 Ofuda (120 coins): seeking paper talismans that burst on hit and fly over walls. Three at once from level 4.
  • 鎖 Kusarigama (150 coins): a chain-blade that circles you. Level 4 adds a second blade, and at level 5 the blades cut enemy arrows out of the air. It keeps spinning even while you charge a cut.

The five skills are Bushidō (+10% damage), Quick hands (attacks 8% faster), Meditation (draw cut charges 12% faster), Iron armor (+1 max HP and heal 1) and Spirit magnet (pull XP from 35% farther and +8% XP). When nothing is left to offer, you'll see Rest (heal 2) or a Coin purse (+15 coins).

Enemies on the road

  • Spearmen walk down alone or in groups of three or four.
  • Archers stop near the top, show a red arc while they draw, and fire a spread of three arrows aimed at you. They fire up to three volleys, then leave.
  • Ninjas appear near the top, right above you, with a red mark. Less than a second later they dash straight down. Step sideways as soon as you see the mark.
  • Bamboo walls cross the road with one gap. Running into a wall costs a hit. A strong draw cut slices through them.

Foes get a little tougher every second (about 1.1% more health per second of the fight), and harder stops spawn them faster too. When the road ends, new enemies stop and you get up to 8 seconds of calm before the boss arrives.

The three bosses

Each boss gains new attacks at 60% and 25% health, and attacks faster each time.

  • 賊 Bandit Chief (Village Outskirts and Rice Terraces): throws fans of knives, sends a rider charging down a red-marked lane where you're standing (two lanes later on), and calls in three spearmen. In the last phase he throws two knife fans at once.
  • 灯 Lantern Mother (Lantern Bridge): sends swarms of small lanterns that weave side to side, fans of fire and a ring of 12 fireballs. From 60% health, columns of fire fall where red marks appear. Below 25%, a full ring of fire replaces the 12-ball ring.
  • 骸 Gashadokuro (The Mountain Gate): red columns that drop three bones each, a slam where a red ring appears under you and a giant hand lands a moment later, and fans of spat bones. From 60% health it summons four skeletons, and below 25% it bursts a ring of bones and slams with two hands.

The village: Forge, Dojo and Shrine

Fights pay four things. Coins drop from foes, plus some for distance and 150 for a win; you keep half if you fall. Weapon scrolls come from each weapon you carry, more the longer you hold it and the higher its level. Spirit comes from perfect cuts. Jade is the slow one: every win pays 3, each new star pays 1 more, and the first clears of Lantern Bridge (+2) and the Mountain Gate (+3) add a bonus.

The Forge. Each weapon has three branches with three ranks each. Ranks cost 5, 12 and 25 of that weapon's scrolls plus 20, 50 and 100 coins. Only the equipped branch works in a fight, and switching is free. The katana's branches are Twin Blades (side waves), Long Reach (waves grow and hit harder) and Counter (waves cut arrows). The others: Piercer, Volley and Fire Arrows for the Yumi; Ricochet, Storm and Poison for the Shuriken; Great Seal, Swarm and Binding for the Ofuda; Wide Orbit, Two Chains and Lash for the Kusarigama. Reach rank 3 in any branch and you can learn that weapon's Mastery (40 scrolls and 200 coins), a small bonus for every fight.

The Dojo. Training costs coins and goes to level 10 in six stats: Strength (+3% damage), Swiftness (+3% attack speed), Vitality (+1 max HP every 2 levels), Focus (draw cut charges 4% faster), Fortune (+5% coins) and Insight (+4% XP). Level 1 costs 40 coins and each level costs 35% more. Draw-cut lessons cost 3, 6 and 10 spirit: Wide Cut (reach), Fast Draw (charge speed), Echo Cut (a weaker second cut follows) and Spirit Cut (cut kills drop extra XP).

The Shrine. One spirit companion fights beside you. The Kitsune is free and throws seeking fox-fire. The Tanuki (5 jade) adds 15% coins and heals you 1 point every 50 seconds. The Crane (8 jade) dives on the toughest foe, or the boss, every few seconds. Levels 2 to 5 cost 2, 3, 4 and 6 jade.

The province map

The first province has seven stops. A stop opens when you clear any stop that leads to it.

  • Village Outskirts (battle): the start. Bandit Chief.
  • Roadside Shrine or Rice Terraces: the shrine offers a choice of two blessings for your next fight; the terraces are a longer, harder battle against a tougher Bandit Chief. Either one opens the next stop.
  • The Wounded Monk (encounter): give him 30 coins for a jade bead, ask him to teach you stillness (the Calm Mind blessing), or walk on.
  • Lantern Bridge or Traveling Merchant: the bridge is an optional elite fight against the Lantern Mother, the hardest stop on the map; the merchant sells blessings. Visiting either opens the last stop.
  • The Mountain Gate (castle): Gashadokuro. Clearing it seals the province.

Blessings last for your next fight only, win or lose. There are five: Healing Tea (+2 max HP), Whetstone (+15% damage), Incense (+25% XP), Reroll Scroll (+2 rerolls) and Calm Mind (draw cut charges 20% faster). Every fight stop has three stars: win, take 2 hits or fewer, and land 5 perfect cuts. Each new star pays 1 jade and 20 coins, and you can replay any fight to collect the ones you missed.

Stances (test)

Under "Combat style" in the village you can try Stances, a test mode. Only one of the katana, bow and shuriken attacks at a time: the katana becomes a sword that slashes whatever comes close, you start with the bow too, and a quick tap of the second finger switches stance. Holding that finger charges the draw cut. Bosses have less health in this mode.

Strategy

Early game: the outskirts

  • Practice the perfect cut from your first fight. In our robot tests on Village Outskirts, a bot that dodged well but never used the draw cut beat the chief only about 4 times in 10. The bot that cut won every time.
  • Unlock a second weapon early. With only the katana unlocked, your cards are katana levels and skills. The Yumi is the cheapest, and its heavy arrows are good against bosses.
  • Against the chief, watch for the red lane. The rider comes down the lane marked where you were standing, so one step sideways is enough.

Middle game: terraces, shrine and monk

  • Take the shrine for a free blessing, then come back to the Rice Terraces for its stars. Both lead to the monk.
  • Scrolls belong to one weapon, so a weapon you carry often funds its own tree. Pick a favorite.
  • Spend early spirit on Wide Cut, which helps every cut reach, including the reach to a boss.

Late game: the bridge and the gate

  • Lantern Bridge is optional. If it's too hard, visit the merchant, buy a Whetstone or Healing Tea, and head for the gate; come back for the bridge's jade later.
  • Against Gashadokuro, keep moving when a red ring appears under you; the hand lands about a second later. Step forward to cut between attacks, then drop back.
  • Jade comes slowly. A first clear with all three stars can pay 6 to 9, and a replay pays 3. The Tanuki and the Crane plus every companion level cost 58 jade in all, so pick one companion to grow first rather than spreading it thin.

Tips

  • Spirit drops vanish after a while or off the bottom of the screen. Spirit magnet pulls them from farther away.
  • Getting hit also clears enemy shots close around you, so one hit rarely turns into two.
  • You can carry several different blessings into one fight.

For parents

  • Cost: Samurai Runner is free. There are no purchases, nothing for sale and no payment screens. Coins and jade are earned only by playing.
  • Ads: the game itself shows no ads.
  • Saving: progress saves in the browser on your device. There's no account and no sign-in. A "start over" option in the village settings erases progress, with a confirmation first.
  • Online: the game loads from our website and a font from Google Fonts. It doesn't send anything about the player anywhere. There's no chat, no leaderboard and no contact with other players.
  • Content: sword fighting against bandits and creatures from Japanese folklore, like a giant skeleton and a one-eyed lantern. When a foe falls it leaves a black ink splash, not blood. The action gets busy. Younger children may find the bosses hard or a little spooky.

FAQ

Is Samurai Runner free?

Yes. It's free to play in your browser, and there's nothing to buy with real money.

Do I need to download anything?

No. Open the game's web page on a phone, tablet or computer and tap "Enter the village". It's built for a phone held upright.

Why does every fight start with just the katana?

On purpose. You grow your arsenal from level-up cards in each fight, while your Forge, Dojo and companion progress carries into every one.

My draw cut can't reach the boss. Is that a bug?

No. Bosses sit high on the screen. Step the ronin forward, cut, then move back. A fully charged cut, or the Wide Cut lesson, reaches farther.

What do I keep if I lose?

Half your coins, all your weapon scrolls and all the spirit from your perfect cuts. A lost fight pays no jade and no stars. Blessings are used up either way.

How do I get jade?

Win fights: every win pays 3 jade, replays included. Each new star pays 1 more, the first clears of Lantern Bridge (+2) and the Mountain Gate (+3) add a bonus, and the wounded monk gives one bead for 30 coins.

Is there more after the Mountain Gate?

Not yet. Clearing it seals the province, and you can replay any fight for stars and rewards. A second province is planned for a later build.

Is there sound?

Yes, quiet sound effects for menus and combat, with no music yet. The Sound button in the corner turns them off, and the game remembers your choice.

Play it now

Samurai Runner runs in your browser on a phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to download. It's an early test, so numbers may change as I learn how people play.

Privacy

Samurai Runner keeps two things in your browser's storage: your save (coins, unlocks, training, companions and map progress) and whether sound is on or off. It doesn't collect personal information and has no accounts, ads or leaderboards. For the details, see our studio privacy policy.
