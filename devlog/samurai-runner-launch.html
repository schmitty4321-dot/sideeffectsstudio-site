Samurai Runner is out: one ronin, one road, one cut

November 12, 2026 · by Dr.Dev

Samurai Runner went live late on November 11 as an early test. It's a phone game you hold upright: a lone ronin walks a mountain road that scrolls toward you, bandits and yokai pour down from the top, and his weapons fire on their own while you steer. At the end of every road a boss is waiting. Within a day, the rest of the painted art and the first sound went in. Here's how it came together.

A second finger for the sword

Games where your weapons fire by themselves are easy to pick up, but they can turn into just moving around. I wanted a real decision every few seconds. So the ronin has a draw cut. One thumb steers. Hold a second finger anywhere and time slows, a ring fills around him, and when you lift the finger he cuts in a wide arc. The longer you hold, the bigger the cut. A strong cut also destroys the arrows and bones in its path and slices through the bamboo walls that cross the road.

The ring flashes white for a short window just after it fills. Lift inside it and you get a perfect cut: double damage, a flash, a 斬 seal, and a head start on the next charge. Perfect cuts also earn spirit, which buys the draw-cut lessons in the village, so the skill you practice is the one that pays.

There's a deliberate risk built in. Bosses sit high on the screen, out of reach of a cut from where you'd normally stand. To hit one you have to step forward into the danger, cut, and fall back. Your other weapons also stop while you hold, so it's always a trade.

Ink, and one color for danger

The game is drawn as black ink on paper. That made one rule easy to keep: red means danger, and nothing friendly is ever red. Enemy arrows, knives, bones, fire, the red lane before a rider charges and the red ring before Gashadokuro's hand comes down are all red. The spirit drops you want to collect are jade green. When the screen gets busy, you only have to watch the red.

Every fight starts with just a katana

Each stop on the map is one fight: a road of roughly two to three minutes, a short calm, then the boss. You begin every fight with only your katana and grow your arsenal from level-up cards as you go, picking one of three each time. What carries over is what you build in the village.

The launch build has five weapons (katana, yumi bow, shuriken, ofuda talismans and the kusarigama chain-blade), each with a three-branch tree at the Forge, five skills, six stats to train at the Dojo, four draw-cut lessons, and three spirit companions at the Shrine: a fox, a tanuki and a crane. The first province has seven stops, including a shrine, a wounded monk who asks something of you, a traveling merchant, and three bosses: the Bandit Chief, the Lantern Mother and Gashadokuro, a giant skeleton from Japanese folklore. Each fight has three stars to earn: win, take two hits or fewer, and land five perfect cuts.

A robot played it first

As with my other games, a robot plays every fight before you do. I run two bots through each stop: one that dodges and picks cards but never cuts, and one that also steps in to cut the boss. On the first stop the bot that cuts wins every time, and the bot that only dodges wins well under half its runs.

The next day: paint and sound

At launch the ronin, the spearmen, archers and ninjas and the armored Bandit Chief were already painted, but the roads, the other bosses and most effects were simple shapes drawn in code. Just after midnight on November 12 the rest of the painted art went in: the one-eyed Lantern Mother, Gashadokuro, the title screen, the village, the province map and the weapon icons. Each stop now has its own painted road, with rice terraces, a bamboo forest for Lantern Bridge and snow for the Mountain Gate. Fallen foes leave black ink splashes, and arrows you cut drop broken onto the road.

Later that day the menus got an ink-and-paper makeover, and the game got its first sound: quiet effects for the menus, cuts, perfect cuts, hits and boss warnings. A Sound button in the corner turns them off. Archers and ninjas also got clearer warnings before they attack.

I also added a test mode called Stances, switched on in the village, where only one of your sword, bow and stars attacks at a time and a quick tap of the second finger switches between them. I'm not sure yet which plays better on a real phone, so both are in.

What's next

This is a prototype, and the numbers will change. The plan for the next big step is more weapons with their own trees, weapon evolutions, and a second province beyond the Mountain Gate. Clearing the gate today seals the first province, and you can replay any fight for its stars. If you play, I'd love to know whether two fingers feel natural to you, or whether the cut should work some other way.
