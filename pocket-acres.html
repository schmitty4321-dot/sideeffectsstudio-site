How to play

In Pocket Acres you do the farm work yourself, from the driver's seat. Every field goes through the same loop, and each step is a short drive.

  1. Drive. Drag on open ground to steer with a floating joystick and let go to brake. You can also tap a spot to travel there on your own, or use the arrow keys or WASD on a computer.
  2. Plow. Drive your tractor over a field of stubble until it's all turned over.
  3. Choose a crop and plant it. On a plowed field, a panel at the top lets you pick a crop and pay for the seed. Then drive over the field again to plant it. Only crops that fit the current season are offered.
  4. Fertilize if you like. While a crop grows, you can pay to fertilize the field and drive over it once more for about 30% more harvest.
  5. Harvest. When the crop is ripe, drive over it to fill your crop tank. A full tank shows "FULL · TAKE TO SILO".
  6. Unload at the silo. Drive to the silo and wait while the load moves across. The yellow arrow always shows you the way.
  7. Sell or make something. Sell from the Market, fill a contract on the Deals screen, or take crops to the City, where buildings turn them into goods worth more.
  8. Grow. Spend your coins on more land, animals, machines, upgrades and city buildings.

Your first 10 minutes

A new farm starts with 200 coins, your tractor, a free Mill in the city and two fields. The Home field already has wheat growing, more than halfway there. The East field is yours too, bare and ready to plow.

A line of text near the top of the screen always tells you the next useful job, and the yellow arrow points to it. Follow it for your first loop: plow the East field, pick a crop, plant it, then harvest the Home field wheat when it ripens. Wheat is a good first crop. It's cheap and quick, ready in a little over one farm day on your first fields.

Time moves fast here. One farm day is two minutes, a season is seven days, and a whole year takes under an hour. So your first harvest comes within minutes.

After that first harvest, short lessons pop up the first time you meet something new: selling, the Market, the Mill and pulling weeds. While crops grow, the guide may send you fishing. Drive onto the dock at the pond and tap Fish here. A four-step lesson shows you how to cast, strike, reel and pull against the fish.

With your first coins, a good next step is the chicken coop for 250 coins. It comes with four hens, and eggs don't take any silo space.

Strategy

Early game: one tractor and a few fields

  • Keep every field busy. A field sitting in stubble earns nothing. When you harvest one, plow and replant it straight away.
  • Use the Mill. Your free Mill turns wheat into flour and corn into cornmeal, popcorn or cereal. Pick a batch size, tap Make, and collect it later. Made goods go into your market stall, not the silo, and sell for more than the crops did.
  • Sell in smaller lots. The price slides down as you sell, so dumping a whole silo at once pays less per item. The market recovers over a day or two, so selling a bit at a time pays better.
  • Buy land early. The Creek field costs 600 and the Orchard field 900. Your first four fields grow faster than the others, so they're the best value.
  • Upgrade the crop tank first. The Bigger crop tank costs 300 for its first level and means fewer trips to the silo. Bigger engine (faster driving) and Wider tools (fewer passes) come next.

Seasons and weather

Each crop can be planted in two seasons. Wheat, corn, strawberries and cotton go in during spring or summer. Pumpkins are summer and autumn. Potatoes and carrots are spring and autumn. Winter wheat, cabbage and garlic are planted in autumn or winter. Hay and trees can go in any time, and the greenhouse grows anything all year.

  • Plant in the best season. Every crop has one best season, such as pumpkins in autumn or cabbage in winter. Planting it then gives about 10% more harvest.
  • Plan for winter. In winter most crops grow at half speed. Winter wheat ignores the cold, and it sells as wheat, which fetches its best price in winter.
  • Watch the storm warning. The guide warns you the day before a storm. A ripe crop still in the field during a storm loses a quarter of its harvest, so bring it in first. Growing crops love the rain and get a head start. Prices also go up for a day or two after a storm.
  • Rotate your crops. Planting the same crop again wears the soil out. The crop picker shows each field's soil as Worn out, Fair or Rich, and what grew there last. A different crop feeds the soil, and hay or trees rest it the most. Rich soil gives a much bigger harvest than worn-out soil.

Middle game: animals, the city and contracts

  • Add animals. The sheep pen (350) makes wool, the cow pasture (700) makes milk, the apple orchard (600) gives apples and the beehives (350) make honey. Animals need feed in the trough, water and a clean pen. A neglected animal sulks and makes less, but it never gets sick. Bees make twice as much honey while your crops are growing.
  • Grow hay for feed. Hay grows in one day and turns into animal feed. The Hay baler (700) rolls it into bales for 50% more feed.
  • Let the herd grow. With two or more cows or sheep, a calf or lamb is born every two seasons, and it grows up two seasons later.
  • Fill the city's six lots wisely. The City has room for six buildings, and the free Mill takes one. The Dairy, Bakery, Spinner, Cannery, Kitchen, Tailor, Sawmill, Candle shop, Straw works, Fish market and Workshop compete for the rest. Match them to what you produce. You can tear one down for half its price back.
  • Take contracts. The Deals screen offers three contracts a day. Each one pays a fixed price above normal, plus a 25% bonus if you deliver on time. You can hold up to three at once.
  • Hire help. The Farmhand (800) can be hired per job to plow, plant or harvest a field for a small fee. The Barn hand (1,200) feeds, waters, cleans and collects from your pens for a few coins.

Late game: the big estate

  • Buy the big fields. The North meadow (1,000) and Big acre (1,300) are much larger than the first fields. The Combine (1,500) harvests wide and holds far more, but it can't cut hay or trees.
  • Haul to town. The Truck (1,000) carries goods to town for 20% more coins. Neighbors also ask for truck deliveries now and then, and they tip more the faster you arrive.
  • Save for the Workshop. At 2,000 it's the priciest city building, and it makes the best goods: sweaters, chairs, books and gift boxes. Each one takes goods from other buildings, such as yarn and felt for a sweater.
  • Plant a forest. With the Tree planter (900) you can turn a field into trees. They take 6 days to grow, then grow back 4 days after each chop, with no new seed. Mushrooms and berries pop up in the forest while it grows. Drive over them to pick them, then sell them for extra coins.
  • Fish the river. Once your fish book has six kinds, the river opens for 2,500 coins. It has five fish you can't find at the lake. Better rods widen the green zone, and the Master rod (4,000) is the best there is.

Tips

  • Market prices move with the seasons and the news. News is heard about the day before it hits prices, so a rumor is a hint.
  • Each good has a peak season. Wool, garlic and cabbage sell best in winter, and strawberries and eggs in summer.
  • Crows land in growing fields and peck away a little of the harvest. Drive close to scare them off.
  • Weeds are optional. Tapping them gives a small extra harvest, and skipping them costs nothing.
  • A runaway cow or a wandering goat sometimes shows up. Drive up to it for a few coins.
  • Fish bite at different times. Some like dawn and dusk, some come out only at night. Read the hints in the fish book.
  • Your first catch of every kind pays a 50-coin bonus. In winter the lake freezes and you fish through an ice hole.
  • Trophy walls let you mount your best fish, from the Log cabin wall (600) to the Mansion trophy hall (8,000).
  • Barn color, tractor paint and fences unlock as you bring in more harvests.

For parents

  • Free, with no purchases. Pocket Acres has no real-money purchases. Coins are earned only by farming and fishing.
  • Ads. The game itself shows no ads.
  • Where progress is saved. The farm is saved in the browser on the device you play on. There are no accounts. A Backup & restore option, behind the Controls button, saves the farm to a file or a code, so you can move it to a new device.
  • Online features. There are none. Prices move as if other farmers in the county were selling too, but those farmers are part of the game. Nothing about your child's play is sent online. The game needs the internet only to load.
  • Chat. There is no chat, no friend list and no contact with other players.
  • Age suitability. It's a calm, cartoon farm with no violence. Animals never get sick or die. Fish are caught with a rod and can be kept, sold or let go. There's a fair amount of reading and some money planning, so younger children may enjoy it more with a grown-up.
  • Vibration. The game can buzz the phone. It's off by default for driving and can be switched off for fishing in the lake Options.

FAQ

Does Pocket Acres cost money?

No. It's free to play in your browser and has no real-money purchases.

I lost my farm. Can I get it back?

Only if you made a backup. Your farm lives in your browser on one device, so clearing the browser's data or playing in a private window loses it. Use Controls, then Backup & restore, to save a file or copy a code now and then.

How do I move my farm to a new phone?

On the old phone, open Controls, then Backup & restore, and tap Save file or Copy code. On the new phone, open the same screen and choose From file or Paste code. The game asks before it replaces the farm on that device.

Can I play on an iPhone?

Yes. Open it in Safari or any other modern browser. It plays best with the phone held upright.

Can I play offline?

You need an internet connection to open the game. There's nothing to install.

What happens while I'm away?

Your farm keeps running for up to three farm days, about six minutes of farm time. Crops ripen, animals make goods and city batches finish. A welcome-back card tells you what's ready. Crows and weeds only turn up while you play.

Why can't I plant corn right now?

Each crop can only be planted in its two seasons, and crops out of season are hidden from the list. Plant something that fits, such as hay or winter wheat in winter, or use the greenhouse, which grows anything all year.

My silo is full. What now?

Sell some goods at the Market, use them in the City, or buy a Bigger silo in the Shop. Made goods, eggs and honey are kept in your market stall instead, which has its own upgrade.

The fish stopped biting. Why?

After a long run of catches the fish need a rest for a while. Head back to the farm and try again later.

Play it now

Pocket Acres runs in your browser on a phone, tablet or computer. There's nothing to download, and your progress saves automatically on your device.
