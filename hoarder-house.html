Hoarder House

One very full house. Clear it, pack it, clean it, one room at a time. Dig through the pile that has buried a family's kitchen, carry each thing to where it belongs, pack every drawer like a puzzle, and scrub the room until it shines.

Early test: Hoarder House went live on November 24, 2026, and it's still being built. Two rooms are open, the furniture is simple stand-in art, and some rules may change after people play it.

How to play

  1. Dig into the pile. Press on the pile and drag. The top thing there comes up, and a panel shows it turning in 3D with its name and where it goes, like "Goes in: Fridge · Middle shelf".
  2. Drop it on the furniture. A piece glows green ✓ (best spot), amber ~ (it fits here) or red ✕ (it doesn't go here). Let go on green or amber and it hops into that piece's waiting tray.
  3. Open the furniture. A 📥 badge shows how many things wait on a piece. Tap the piece: its doors swing open and its drawers and shelves appear as grids.
  4. Pack it like a puzzle. Every item has a real shape: a fork takes two squares, a frying pan is a square with a handle. Turn and fit them until each drawer locks with a cha-ching.
  5. Find the heirloom hidden at the bottom of every pile.
  6. Clean up. Once the floor is clear and every tray is empty, the cleaning tools appear. When every surface sparkles, you get your grade.

The top bar shows the room, how many things are left in the pile, a clock of how long you've been tidying (there's no time limit) and the 🏆 Scores button. The Room and Pile buttons zoom the view out to the whole room or in on the pile.

Your first 10 minutes

There's no title screen. The game opens in the kitchen: a fridge and freezer, cabinets, drawers, a sink, a dishwasher, a stove and a recipe shelf, with 125 things heaped on the floor. A tutorial card walks you through five steps, with a pointing hand or a ring, and you can skip it.

  1. Dig in: drag something onto the piece the panel names.
  2. Put it away: it waits on that piece under a 📥 badge. Tap the piece.
  3. Pack it in: drag it from the tray into a drawer, tapping it to turn it.
  4. Fill drawers for stars: a full drawer locks. Tap Done to go back to the room.
  5. Find the heirloom: Grandma wants her Golden Spork back before Sunday soup.

Later, short tips explain the carry basket, Auto-fit and moving the view. A good first goal is the cutlery drawer: it's small and quick to lock.

Clearing the pile

You can only take the top thing of each stack, and digging next to a tall stack can make it slide over ("The pile slumped!"). A tap without dragging tells you an item's name. Each piece's waiting tray holds a quarter more than its drawers. When it's full, the piece refuses with "tray full, organize it first".

  • Carry basket (by the door): things for other rooms, like a lost sock, a TV remote or a dog leash. The panel says "For the Laundry room · carry basket". It refuses anything that belongs in this room.
  • Donate box and Toss bag: for real throwaways, like the old takeout menus, the twist ties, the chipped mug and the fourth extra spatula. Open the box or bag, drag them in and tap Empty it.
  • Loose junk: tap or flick the crumpled bags and boxes around the pile and they fly into the toss bag. It's optional.

The first time you lift the heirloom, the game stops for a reveal with its picture, its story and where it goes. Then it's back on top for you to carry. It can't be given away.

Packing drawers

The organize screen shows the piece's drawers beside the waiting tray (on an upright phone, above it). The bottom row, marked FRONT, is the front of the drawer.

  • Move: drag a piece into a grid. The cells it would cover light up first. Drop it on exactly one other item and they swap. Drag a piece back to the tray to take it out.
  • Turn: tap a piece. While holding one, a second finger, the mouse wheel, a right-click, R or ↻ Turn also turn it. Everything turns all four ways and stays where you put it.
  • Lock: each drawer shows a count like "8 / 12 · locks at 11". It locks at 85% of its cells, rounded up, so a 6-cell space must be completely full. A whole piece locked plays a fanfare and flashes gold in the room.
  • Auto-fit ×3: fills one drawer with waiting things whose best home it is (the selected item's drawer, if you picked one). Three per room; a tap that places nothing is free.
  • 🎁 Donate sends the selected waiting item out of the house. ↩ To floor puts it back on the floor to carry elsewhere.

Locked drawers can be reopened. Take something out and the drawer unlocks until you fill it again.

Stars and bonus goals

Each drawer and shelf earns one star for locking, two if more than half of its filled cells are best-home things (green ✓, not amber ~), and three if it also meets every bonus goal under its name. A goal turns from ○ to ✓ when you meet it:

  • Rainbow rows: coloured things run red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink from left to right in each row.
  • Small to big: each row goes from small on the left to big on the right.
  • Matching category, material or pattern: three or more touching things share it.
  • Daily items up front and Treasures up front: everyday things, or the rarest thing, touch the front row.
  • Complete set: every piece of a set is together.
  • Oldest first and Owners together: soonest best-by first, and each person's things side by side. The game doesn't show best-by dates or owners yet, so for now these two are mostly luck.

Cleaning up

The grime is there from the start, but the tools only appear once the pile is gone and every waiting tray is empty. A checklist shows each surface, the icon of the tool it needs and how clean it is. Pick a tool and rub. With a tool in hand, a quick tap on a surface flies the camera close and makes the right tool glow, and two fingers move the view.

  • Mop: the floor, two passes.
  • Spray, then cloth: sticky counter tops, the dishwasher door and the cutting boards.
  • Cloth: the fridge door, cabinet doors, drawer fronts, spice rack and the pantry's showcase shelf.
  • Sponge: the stove top (about three passes) and the sink (two).
  • Duster: the upper cabinets, the recipe shelf and the pantry shelves.

The wrong tool gets a hint about the right one, and the last specks clean themselves, so you never hunt for a dirty spot. The kitchen has 12 surfaces, the pantry 3.

Your grade

A finished kitchen shows a before-and-after picture you can wipe across, then a score page. Each star is worth 10 points. Each real item given away, with Donate or as a keeper in the donate box, costs 5; the throwaways cost nothing. Your grade is your share of the most points possible: 95% is an A+, 90% an A, down to D− at 40%. The kitchen has 66 stars to win, the pantry 21. Press On to the Pantry to move on; if you closed the page, the cleaning checklist's "See before & after" brings it back.

Strategy

The kitchen

  • Put away as you go. The fridge takes a lot of the pile, and opening a piece when its badge grows saves you "tray full" refusals.
  • Big pieces first: place the stockpot, frying pan, egg carton and cookie sheet before small things fill the gaps.
  • Save Auto-fit for awkward spaces like the long pots cabinet (7 by 2) or the dishwasher's bottom rack (7 by 4).
  • The recipe shelf is Grandma's Baking Corner. It locks only with all five baking things: the cookbook, teapot, cookie jar, rolling pin and Golden Spork. Put the spork in the front row for "Treasures up front".

The pantry

  • 60 things and seven spaces: three shelves, a salty and a sweet snack bin, the bulk floor space and a showcase shelf.
  • The showcase shelf holds two sets, Pantry Classics (beans, soup, tuna, cereal) and Toast Toppings (honey, jam, peanut butter, maple syrup), and locks only when completely full.
  • The heirloom is the Golden Pineapple, which Dad hid as a joke. It belongs on the middle shelf.
  • A tea tin, a tub of mayo, frozen waffles and yogurt tubes belong in the kitchen, so they go in the carry basket.

Keeping your grade up

  • Most kitchens end with a few extras, because some drawers are the best home for more than they hold. Before donating one, try To floor and carry it to another piece that glows amber.
  • Only throwaways belong in the donate box: a keeper in it costs 5 points too.
  • Amber things help a drawer lock, but too many stop it reaching two stars.

Tips

  • Pressing on the pile always grabs something. To slide the view, start on the floor, walls or furniture, or use two fingers.
  • Carry something to the edge of the screen and the view slides that way.
  • Free cam lets you swing the camera around; tap it again (now Fixed cam) to go back.
  • Tapping the stove or the carry basket shows a card instead; the basket's card lists what's in it.
  • On a computer, Escape closes the organize screen and R turns the selected piece.

For parents

  • Cost: Hoarder House is free. There are no purchases, no coins for sale and no payment screens.
  • Ads: the game shows no ads.
  • Tone: a cozy tidying game about a family whose house got too full. The stories are gentle, like Grandma's lucky spork and Dad's joke pineapple, and nobody is laughed at. It rewards finding everything a home; donating is a kind way out for extras.
  • No pressure: there's no time limit and nothing to lose.
  • Saving: progress saves automatically in the browser on your device. There's no account and no sign-in. Clearing the browser's site data deletes the save, and a save doesn't move between devices.
  • Online: the game loads from our website, and its 3D engine (the free three.js library) loads from the public unpkg.com code server. Item models download as they appear, a few dozen megabytes in all, so the first visit on mobile data may be slow. The game sends nothing about the player anywhere. There's no chat and no contact with other players.
  • Sound: short effects like a cha-ching, chimes and a mop's slosh. No music, and no mute button yet, so use the device's volume.
  • Reading: item names and the tutorial are text, so younger children may want help. Colours always come with ✓, ~ or ✕ for colour-blind players.
  • Early test: the game is new, still changing and hasn't been tried on many phones. It's 3D, so an older phone may run it slowly.

FAQ

Is Hoarder House free?

Yes. It's free to play in your browser, and there's nothing to buy with real money.

Do I need to download anything?

No. Open the game's web page on a phone, tablet or computer and the kitchen loads.

Where is my progress saved?

In your browser, on the device you play on. It saves every few seconds and when you switch away. If you clear the browser's data for the site, the save is gone.

I dropped something on the fridge and it disappeared. Where did it go?

It's waiting on the fridge's tray. Things only show inside furniture once you've packed them. Tap the fridge to put things away.

Everything is locked but things are still waiting. What now?

That piece has more things than space. Use To floor and carry an extra to another piece that glows amber, or tap 🎁 Donate (5 points for a real item).

What happens to the things in the carry basket?

For now they wait there. The plan is for them to turn up in their own rooms when those rooms open, but that isn't built yet.

What happens after the pantry?

For now, that's the end of the early test. The plan is the rest of the house, room by room: the dining room, living room, hallway and on to the attic and the shed.

How do I start over?

There's no button for it yet. Clearing the browser's site data for the game starts a fresh kitchen, but it also erases your grades.

Can I play with my phone upright?

Yes. The rooms fit an upright or sideways screen, and an upright organize screen puts the drawers above the tray.

Play it now

Hoarder House runs in your browser on a phone, tablet or computer. There's nothing to download, and your progress saves automatically on your device.
